# Nurseries and childcare: vertical brief

Researched 18 Sep 2026 as part of the five-title wave (farming, gym/fitness,
nurseries, retirement villages, dental). Same structure as the fleet, golf,
barbering and airports briefs: market, competitors, demand, advertisers, risk,
verdict. Scope: a business title for nursery owners and operators, childminders,
pre-schools and out-of-school clubs (funding rates, the 30-hours expansion,
staffing and ratios, Ofsted, fees, occupancy, M&A, property, software, food,
equipment). UK-first.

## 0. Verdict

**Conditional yes, but not a lead pick. The money is real (£9.5bn of state
funding in 2026-27, a busy M&A market, a sector restructuring around the
30-hours expansion) and the commercial SERPs are held by vendors, brokers and
content farms rather than any publisher. But the one business-first incumbent,
Nursery Management Today, already occupies the owner-operator seam commercially,
the usable wire is thin (about 2 to 4 items a day against a target of 7), and the
editorial risk is the highest of any vertical we have assessed: this is a sector
whose biggest stories are about harm to children.**

Scored 57/100 on the wave rubric (§11). It works only as a narrow, owner-facing
money title under a hard safeguarding rule, and it should launch behind any
wave candidate that scores higher on supply and risk.

Four findings drive the conditional yes.

1. **The state is now the main customer, and the money is moving.** The
   government expects to provide **over £9.5bn for the early years entitlements in
   2026-27**, more than double the 2023-24 commitment, after the working-parent
   entitlement completed its roll-out to children from 9 months in September 2025.
   Funding rates rose to a national average of **£6.42/hr (3-4s), £8.90 (2s) and
   £12.04 (under 2s)**, with a 54% spread between local authorities. Every rate,
   every pass-through rule and every late payment is a business story for an
   owner.
2. **Consolidation is accelerating and the deals are named.** Christie & Co
   counts **15,090 UK day nurseries delivering 870,454 places**; large groups (21+
   settings) took **62% of completed deals in 2025 and 61% in H1 2026**; prices
   rose **3.8%** in 2025; **75% of operators plan to buy, sell or both** in 2026.
   Kids Planet completed ~40 acquisitions in 2025 to overtake Bright Horizons.
   Private equity backs only **6.8%** of settings, so the roll-up has a long way to
   run. Nobody publishes a free, live deal ledger.
3. **The money SERPs are undefended.** "How to open a nursery", "how much is a
   nursery worth", "nursery occupancy rates", "nursery management software",
   "early years funding rates by local authority", "nursery staff ratios": every
   one is held by software vendors, business brokers, accountants, training
   farms, a review site or government. **Neither Nursery World nor NMT ranks on
   any of them.**
4. **The advertisers are proven and mostly shared across the incumbents.** NMT
   lists **35 paying partners** (Famly, Blossom, Connect Childcare, Morton Michel,
   Christie & Co, Savills, Community Playthings, Apetito, YPO, Hope Education...);
   four UK shows sell **roughly 410 stands a year** before dedup (Childcare &
   Education Expo London 130+, Expo National 100+, Nursery Management Show 100,
   Nursery World's Big Day Out 80).

The three things that could kill it, in order:

- **Safeguarding is the sector's lead story, and it is radioactive for an
  AI-written title.** This week alone: Ofsted's second legal notice to Bright
  Horizons covering 39 settings (after the Vincent Chan case, sentenced 12 Feb
  2026), and a council-authorised prosecution over a two-year-old's choking death
  at a Radlett nursery. The government is tripling unannounced inspections and
  weighing mandatory CCTV. We must cover the business consequences without ever
  covering the harm (§9).
- **Consumer contamination is the worst we have measured.** Bing's related-term
  data puts operator intent at **~1% of the "childcare" seed and ~12% of the
  "nursery" seed**, and most of that 12% is existing customers logging into one
  software product. The bare `nursery` news feed is ~25% usable; `childcare` is
  under 10% (§4).
- **The seam is not empty, it is under-served.** NMT (Nexus Media) is explicitly
  "for nursery owners and managers", runs an Owners Club, a 100-exhibitor show
  and national awards, and has 35 paying partners. Its weakness is web output
  (2 to 3 news items a week, not in Tranco's list at all), not commercial reach.
  We would be the web-first, daily, data-led alternative on the same advertiser
  list, which is a harder sell than barbering's empty field.

| Age band | National average | Range |
|---|---|---|

2026-27 also moves every funding stream to a termly adjustment system, which is a
cashflow story for every provider. NDNA's survey: **92% of nurseries say their
rates do not cover costs**, and 85% expected to lose money on each funded place.
Provider-reported average charges (SCEYP 2025) sit at £7.18 (under 2s), £7.09
(2s), £6.78 (3-4s). Scotland (1,140 funded hours, Care Inspectorate), Wales
(Flying Start, Childcare Offer) and Northern Ireland run different systems: every
figure must name its nation (§9).

### The deal market

- Christie & Co Business Outlook 2026: 2025 was one of its busiest years on
  record, with sellers racing the Business Asset Disposal Relief rise (14% to 18%
  from 6 April 2026). Average price up 3.8%. Leasehold share of deals up to 73%.
- Buyer mix 2025: large groups 62%, medium groups (3-20 settings) 17%, small
  groups/first-time buyers 21%. Large groups buy bigger sites (84 places vs 49).
- Christie Finance: **48.4% more providers seeking funding** year on year.
- Valuations: brokers quote **4x to 7x EBITDA**, plus price per registered place.
- Largest groups (July 2026): **Busy Bees 360, Kids Planet 301, Bright Horizons
  253, Bright Stars 144**; Storal 5,834 places. Savills: 71% of nurseries in the
  30 largest groups are leasehold.
- Live deals in the last 30 days that our feeds surfaced: Granby Nurseries 9 to 13
  settings (Lincolnshire), Kids Planet two Cheshire settings, Nursery Elite buying
  two-site Sunflowers (Birmingham), a Christie-brokered Oxfordshire sale.

### Pain points with citable numbers

- Staffing is ~74-75% of operating cost *(broker/accountant estimate; unverified)*;
  settings carry ~4 FTE vacancies each and 57% of staff are thinking of leaving
  *(secondary source citing surveys; unverified)*. DfE estimated 35,000 extra
  staff needed for the Sept 2025 expansion.
- Closures: NDNA counted 199 nursery closures Sept 2023 to Sept 2024 on top of
  216 the year before, 38% in the most deprived 30% of areas.
- School-based nursery expansion: a further **331 schools** funded (£150k each,
  £45m pot) to open or expand nurseries from Sept 2026, 6,000+ places. This is a
  competitive threat to private operators and a beat in its own right.
- Safeguarding reform: Ofsted to inspect new providers within 18 months, move to
  at least once every four years, triple unannounced visits; free universal
  safeguarding training (with NSPCC); chain-level reporting; an expert group on
  mandatory CCTV. All of it lands on operators' cost lines.

### Calibration and the honest read

Fleet's incumbents were 2.5-2.8m and we beat them; golf clustered 458k-2.5m;
Health Club Management at 340k was too strong to attack; airports ran 102k-645k.
**Nursery World at 466k sits in the golf band: beatable on the web, but it is the
only title with real authority and it owns the practitioner and policy space.**
NMT is invisible to search. Famly (54k) is stronger than every publisher in the
sector and uses that strength for content marketing, as Booksy did in barbering.

The seam, therefore: **the nursery as a business, written for the person who
signs the lease and the payroll.** Funding-rate economics, fees and occupancy,
deal-making and valuations, property, staffing cost, compliance cost, and the
supplier landscape. NMT holds this seam in print and at events; nobody holds it
on the web, daily, with data. Pedagogy (EYFS practice, activities, child
development) belongs to Nursery World, EYE, Teach Early Years and eyfs.info. We
do not contest it.

Same three consequences as golf and airports: the SERP is winnable exactly where

SERP is a prospective advertiser; and neither incumbent publisher competes for
commercial queries. The difference from airports: **the absolute volume is
tiny.** Bing returned zero related rows for "nursery staff ratios" and pure noise
for "nursery management software" and "how to start a nursery". The prize is a
few thousand high-value searches a month, not tens of thousands.

**Read:** for every operator search there are roughly 8 to 100 parent searches
depending on the seed, and the operator searches that do exist are
disproportionately navigational (logins to software they already use). Worse
than barbering (style terms), comparable to golf's raw feed. The one clean
cluster is regulatory: "ofsted framework 2026", "ofsted toolkit 2026", "eyfs
2026", "early years inspection toolkit" are practitioner/manager terms with no
parent twin, but gov.uk owns the answers.

### The operator-intent filter (for the Researcher and the keyword registry)

Keep a term only if it contains an operator marker: *provider, setting, owner,
manager, funding rate, rate per hour (LA), pass-through, ratio, occupancy, fees
increase (as provider), EBITDA, valuation, for sale, buy a nursery, acquisition,
lease, business rates, NMW / national living wage, payroll, staff cost,
recruitment, Level 3, qualification, software, CCTV, Ofsted framework /
toolkit / notification / registration, SEF, DBS, insurance.*

Drop any term containing: *near me, free childcare, tax-free childcare, login,
apply, eligibility, how much will I pay, rhymes, garden/plant/tree, furniture,
decor, cot, baby, best nursery in, reviews, term dates.*

> **The buyer rule (nursery edition).** Every article is written for someone who
> is paid to run a setting: owner, director, nursery manager, childminder as a
> business, pre-school committee chair, out-of-school club operator, group ops or
> finance lead, investor, lender, broker, supplier. If the natural reader is a
> parent, it is not our article. No "how to choose a nursery", no "how to claim 30
> hours", no child-development tips, no named-nursery rankings for parents.
> Parents' behaviour enters our pages only as demand data.

> **Never seed the engine with the bare queries `nursery`, `childcare`, `day
> nursery`, `nursery staff`, `early years sector` or `30 hours childcare`.**
> Scoped queries only (§5). And never let the word "nursery" in a feed item stand
> alone: plant nurseries (Wyevale, Costa Farms, MANTS), hospital neonatal
> nurseries (the Pakistan PIMS fire) and fish/oyster nurseries all come through
> the same query.

## 6. Audience, the honest sizing

- **27,900 non-domestic childcare providers** in England (Ofsted), of which
  **~13,000 day nurseries** (Christie). DfE counts 21,400 group-based providers.
  31% of group-based providers sit in a chain, so the owner/decision-maker count
  is well below the setting count: plausibly **10,000 to 15,000 distinct
  business owners** plus group head offices *(estimate from the chain share;
  unverified)*.
- **24,700 childminders**, each a sole trader, shrinking ~2% a year: overlaps
  Smart SME's reader profile exactly, but small spenders.
- **9,900 school-based providers** (maintained nursery schools and school nursery
  classes): a public-sector reader with a budget-holder, and the target of the
  school-based nursery expansion.
- **353,700 paid staff**, of whom managers and deputies are the realistic
  second-tier reader (roughly one manager per setting).
- The supply chain: software, food, furniture, insurance, recruitment, training,
  brokers, lenders, landlords, PE investors.
- Scotland (981 nurseries), Wales (746), NI (326) plus Ireland (a live pay and
  funding story this week) extend the pool modestly.

**Reader definition to lock before launch:** owner / director · group
ops/finance/M&A lead · nursery manager · pre-school chair or manager ·
childminder running it as a business · out-of-school club operator · investor,
lender, broker · supplier sales and BD.

Compared with the fleet: a clearer UK B2B universe than golf or airports, smaller
than Smart SME, similar in shape to barbering (many small owner-operators, a
consolidating top tier) but with far more state money and regulation.

## 8. Tools we can build

Same primitive as golf and airports: a structured entity database with scheduled
enrichment. Ranked by "creates a proprietary, citable number".

1. **Funding Rate Tracker (build first).** All 151 English LAs x three age bands,
   plus pass-through, supplements, payment timing and the termly adjustment; Wales,
   Scotland and NI alongside. Owners search their own LA every year. Only Clear
   Nursery Fees (unranked, parent-facing) and council PDFs exist. Directly answers
   the §3 query nobody owns.
2. **Nursery Deal Tracker.** Every acquisition: buyer, seller, settings, places,
   adviser, date. Christie publishes its own deals; nobody publishes the market.
   Feeds the news desk and is the natural backlink target for every broker and
   lawyer.
3. **Group League Table, live.** Ofsted's monthly management information lists
   registered providers and places; rolling it up by group gives a monthly league
   table where Nursery World publishes once a year *(feasibility of group
   attribution from Ofsted MI unverified)*.
4. **Cost-per-funded-hour calculator.** Staff cost by ratio band, NMW/NLW, pension,
   rent, versus the local rate: the "does this place lose money" number every
   owner argues about. Uses the cogent-base tools framework.
5. **Nursery valuation calculator.** EBITDA multiple range and price-per-place from
   the deal tracker. Broker bait and broker advertiser magnet.
6. **Occupancy and fee index.** Quarterly, from provider surveys or published
   fees *(method not yet designed)*.

Deliberately not built: any Ofsted rating ranking of named nurseries (that is a
parent product, and a safeguarding-adjacent one).

## 9. Editorial risk

Five rules. The first two are load-bearing; this is the riskiest vertical we have
assessed.

1. **The buyer rule** (§4).
2. **The safeguarding rule (new, and stricter than airports' incident rule).** We
   do not cover abuse, injury, death or neglect of children as news. Ever. No
   named children, no named accused individuals, no named individual settings in
   connection with harm. Regulatory enforcement is covered only at **group or
   policy level**, only from official sources (Ofsted, DfE, gov.uk notices, court
   outcomes after verdict), and only for its business consequence: what a
   chain-level welfare notice means for operators, what mandatory CCTV would cost,
   how insurance premiums move. **Live prosecutions (for example the Radlett case)
   are off-limits entirely** because of contempt-of-court risk. Any draft that
   mentions a child's death, abuse or a criminal case goes to a human before
   publish, with no auto-publish path. This goes into `docs/editorial-standard.md`
   and the engine's pre-publish checks before the first draft.
3. **The no-children-imagery rule.** No AI-generated images of children, and no
   stock photos of identifiable children. Buildings, empty rooms, outdoor spaces,
   adults at work, data graphics. For an AI-run title, one synthetic image of a
   toddler in a news context is a reputational event.
4. **The nation-and-scope figure rule.** Funding, ratios, entitlements and
   regulators differ across England, Scotland, Wales, NI and Ireland; LA rates
   vary 54%; market-size figures diverge by definition (day nurseries only vs all
   childcare). Every figure carries nation, source, year and scope, or a range.
5. **The politics rule.** Funding is a live political fight (Labour's expansion,
   the Welsh Flying Start budget red line, the IFS and think-tank "cliff edge"
   work). Cover it as economics: rates, costs, margins, capacity. No party line.

YMYL: moderate. Child welfare adjacency means Google will judge the site on trust
signals; named human editors and an editorial policy page are a launch
requirement, not a nice-to-have.

### Visual requirement

Warm but businesslike: closer to barbering's softer palette than airports' data
density, but the §8 tools need real tables. No child photography (rule 3) forces
an illustration-and-architecture visual language, which also differentiates us
from the parent sites.

## 10. Naming and domains

as available). No trademark search done; the manual check is JB's step, from a

**Two naming hazards specific to this sector:**

- **"Nursery" collides with horticulture.** *Nursery Management* is a US
  plant-nursery trade magazine (nurserymag.com); MANTS, Wyevale Nurseries and

**Recommendation: Early Years Business (earlyyearsbusiness.co.uk primary, .com

horticulture problem and excludes childminders and pre-school committees.

## 12. Before committing

1. **One named advertiser test, as with every title.** The obvious first calls are
   the vendors who pay NMT and would want a web-first, search-visible alternative:
   Famly (whose own content marketing proves it buys search), Christie & Co (deal
   tracker sponsorship, and a care-plus-childcare package with Care Home
   Magazine), Morton Michel (Expo headline sponsor). If none will take a launch
   slot, park the title.
2. **Decide the safeguarding rule and the human-review gate before anything
   else.** If the engine cannot guarantee that no draft touching a child's harm
   auto-publishes, do not launch.
3. **Register earlyyearsbusiness.co.uk and .com** (JB's step), trademark check by
   phone first. Optionally watch nurserybusiness.com through its pending-delete
   drop, as a bonus not a plan.
4. **Build the supply before the site.** A `NURSERY_NEWS_SEARCHES` block of scoped
   queries only (never the bare terms in §4), plus gov.uk Atom feeds for Ofsted
   and DfE, NDNA, EYA, Christie & Co, Insider Media and TheBusinessDesk as company
   hubs. Re-measure after two weeks: if sector-grade supply is still under 3/day,
   the daily target drops to what the wire supports plus tools-led features.
5. **Seed the Funding Rate Tracker as the launch flagship.** DfE publishes all 151
   LA rates; this is the fastest route to owning an operator query.
6. **Keyword registry**: add the §4 operator filter and exclude list, and
   register nursery-business terms against Smart SME so the two titles do not
   cannibalise small-business-in-childcare pieces.
