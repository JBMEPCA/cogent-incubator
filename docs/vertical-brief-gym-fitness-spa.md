# Gym, fitness and spa — vertical brief

Title #3 candidate, researched 17 Aug 2026. Same structure as the fleet business
case: market, competitors, demand, advertisers, risk, verdict.

## 0. Verdict

**Yes to fitness. No to spa. Do not run them as one title.**

The gym/studio/PT end of the market is a better commercial fit than fleet was, and
~~the *only* publication positioned where we would sit has been dormant since October
2025.~~ **[Corrected 18 Sep 2026: Gym Owner Monthly was never dormant. It moved to
gymownermonthly.com and has published ~10 items a month all year. It is still
unranked and press-release led, so the seam is contested but weakly held. See
"Refresh, 18 Sep 2026" below.]** But the sector's top incumbent is roughly eight times stronger than the fleet
incumbents we chose to attack, so this is a bottom-of-market entry, not a
head-on one — and the editorial risk profile is materially worse than fleet's.

Spa fails on three counts independently: separate readers, separate advertisers,
and an audience where one business in five is trading at a loss.

## 1. The market

### Fitness

| Metric | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Health and fitness clubs | 5,842 | +4.2% YoY |
| Members (16+) | 12.2m | 18% penetration, a record |
| Sector income | £6.5bn | up from £5.7bn |
| Visits | 679m | +10% since 2024 |
| Clubs run by the top 10 operators | 1,429 | up from 1,365 |

PureGym 422 sites, The Gym Group 244, Anytime Fitness 177. Source: the UK Health
& Fitness Market Report 2026, commissioned by ukactive.

Two things follow from that table. First, ad budgets follow growth and this sector
is at a record — it is a better moment to enter than fleet was. Second, the count
of *club operators* is small: 5,842 sites, a quarter of them inside ten head
offices. A title aimed only at club operators has a reader universe in the low
thousands.

The audience only becomes big when you include the people the incumbent does not
serve well:

- **25,000+ registered Level 3 personal trainers**, ~60% freelance or
  self-employed.
- **91% of enterprises in UK sport and physical activity employ fewer than 49
  people.**

That second figure is the whole argument. This is structurally a small-business
sector wearing a corporate-looking trade press, and small business is the muscle
Smart SME already has.

### Spa and wellness

| Metric | Latest | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Health and wellness spa businesses | 3,651 | +4.7% YoY (IBISWorld) |
| Sector size | £8.1bn (2025, IBISWorld) | market-research figures vary widely |

A sector where a fifth of the businesses are loss-making and three quarters are
sub-five-employee is a poor advertising market. Suppliers know it. This is the
opposite of the fleet picture, where the reader controls a five-figure fleet
budget.

market.

## 3. Who actually owns the searches

This is the finding that changes the strategy, and it did not show up in fleet.

Searching the sector's core commercial B2B queries returns **software vendors, not
publishers**. Across "gym management software UK", "gym marketing ideas", "fitness
business ideas", "SEO for gyms", the results were Xplor Gym, TeamUp, PushPress,
Resamania, WodGuru, GymRoute, Exercise.com, Capterra and GetApp — with Health Club
Management essentially absent.

Two consequences:

1. **The SERP is winnable.** None of those vendor blogs has domain strength —
   xplorgym.co.uk is not in the Tranco top million. They rank on topical pages
   against publishers who are not competing for the query at all. Editorial that
   actually targets these terms has room.
2. **Every one of them is a prospective advertiser.** A vendor investing in a
   content-marketing programme has a demonstrated budget and a demonstrated belief
   in this audience. The competitor set and the advertiser list are the same list.

**The contamination risk is the important part.** In fleet, "BiK rate 2026/27" has
no consumer twin. Here, almost every term does: "gym near me" runs at roughly
100,000 searches a month and "24-hour gym" around 50,000. If the engine drifts
toward consumer fitness it will be competing with Men's Health on a domain with no
authority, and losing. **The demand map must be filtered to business-intent queries
before it goes anywhere near the Researcher**, and the standard must forbid the
drift explicitly.

## 5. Editorial risk — worse than fleet, and it needs its own rule

Fleet's load-bearing rule was the figure rule, because a wrong BiK percentage is
somebody's five-figure decision. This vertical needs two rules, because the failure
mode is regulatory rather than financial.

1. **The scope rule.** We cover the *business* of fitness, never the *practice* of
   it. No training advice, no nutrition or supplement guidance, no weight-loss
   pharmacology (GLP-1 in gyms is a live sector story and must be covered as a
   business and liability story only), no treatment efficacy. An AI-written title
   with no named clinician has no business in health advice, and Google's YMYL
   standards make it unwinnable ground anyway.
2. **The claims rule.** Every health, results, saving or performance claim is
   attributed to the party making it and never asserted in our voice. The spa and
   aesthetics end additionally sits under ASA/CAP rules — prescription-only
   injectables cannot lawfully be advertised to the public at all — which is a
   second reason to keep the spa side out of the initial masthead.

Both are cheap to enforce in the editorial standard, and both are permanent.

## 6. Advertisers

The strongest part of the case, and stronger than fleet's.

**Evidence of a market that already spends:**

- **Elevate**, Excel London, 17–18 June 2026, tenth edition: **~200 exhibitors**
  across equipment, software, flooring, supplements, physio and rehab, sportswear

  the World Spa & Wellness Convention.
- **Health Club Management sells the product we would sell** — "featured supplier"
  placements and paid press-release slots sit throughout its site. That is a

## 7. Recommendation

**Launch a gym/studio/PT *business* title. Leave spa out.**

Why spa is excluded rather than deferred inside the same masthead:

- Different readers (spa managers and hotel groups, not gym operators), different
  advertisers (product houses, not equipment and software), different shows.
- Two competent incumbents already there — Professional Beauty at 353,024 plus
  Scratch, the NHBF and the Guild — where fitness has one strong incumbent aimed
  at a different tier than us.
- The worst margin picture in the set, and therefore the worst ad market.
- The highest regulatory exposure.

If spa ever runs, it runs as its own title, and its wedge is the hotel spa P&L —
Spa Business at 588,047 and European Spa outside the top million mean the *online*
spa-business ground is genuinely weak. That is a real opportunity, just not this
one, and not attached to this masthead.
