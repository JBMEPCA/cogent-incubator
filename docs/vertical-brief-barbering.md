# Barbering, UK-first — vertical brief

**DECIDED 21 Aug 2026: JB approved the launch. The title is "Barbering
Business" (barberingbusiness.co.uk / .com — both confirmed free at decision
time). Launch tracking: `docs/barbering-business-launch.md`.**

Title #4 candidate, researched 21 Aug 2026 at JB's request. Same structure as the
fleet, gym/fitness and golf briefs: market, competitors, demand, advertisers,
risk, verdict. JB's steer: a business tool for barbers, barbershop owners and
male grooming businesses — profitability, marketing, booking, products — with a
visual identity that fits the industry, **much less data-heavy than the current
builds**. Note: CIM's existing grooming title is **Dog Grooming** (pet), which
matters commercially — see §7.

## 0. Verdict

**Yes — launch it. The web whitespace is the widest of the four verticals we
have assessed, the advertiser list is proven by event-sponsorship money already
being spent, and the Dog Grooming crossover gives us the same internal-asset
argument that carried golf.**

Four findings drive that.

1. **No barbering-specific publication on earth has a ranked domain.** Modern
   Barber, BarberEVO, BarberNV, The Barber Magazine, BarberSociety, The Barber
   Today — every one is absent from Tranco's entire ranked list (checked 20 Aug
   2026). The only ranked domains near the sector are general hairdressing
   (BehindTheChair ~247k, HJ ~486k) and the trade body (NHBF ~1.32M). The
   incumbents compete on free print distribution and Instagram; the vertical's
   search audience is unserved. That is precisely where our engine plays.
2. **The closest incumbent is retreating from publishing.** Modern Barber
   (Professional Beauty Group) killed its print edition with the Oct–Dec 2024
   issue, went "fully digital for 2026", and its awards are being absorbed into
   HJ's British Hairdressing Business Awards from 2027. PBG is consolidating the
   brand into events, not publishing. BarberNV is dormant with its archive
   deleted; The Barber Magazine is dead (last posts Nov 2023).
3. **The advertisers are already spending, and we can name them.** The Modern
   Barber Awards 2025 sponsor list (American Crew, Andis, BaBylissPRO, Gamma+,
   Matakki, Takara Belmont, Vagaro), Barber Connect's headline sponsor (Andis),
   Wahl funding its own national awards, and BarberEVO's charity-ride sponsors
   (Captain Fawcett, Barbicide, Reuzel, Uppercut Deluxe, Wahl, Denman…) are a
   ready-made buyer list — proven trade ad spend, not inferred.
4. **The sector is the fastest-growing category on the UK high street** —
   ~18,411 shops, up 50% since 2018, ~99% independent — with a strong recurring
   policy beat (VAT, employment status, the organised-crime crackdown) that no
   incumbent covers in a business-first way.

The three things to design in before launch, not after: **consumer
contamination worse than fitness** (six-figure monthly style terms), a **reader
whose margins are being squeezed** (one in five trading at a loss — shapes tone
and content, not viability, since revenue is advertiser-side), and the fact
that **our advertiser list is again our SEO competitor list** (Booksy-class
content budgets plus a wholesaler buying-guide layer). §4, §9 and §12 carry
those.

## 1. The market

### Footprint — the headline strength

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| UK barbershops (2025) | **~18,411** | The Times / Observer, May 2025 |
| Growth since 2018 | **+50%**; 750+ new openings in 2024 alone | same |
| Five-year unit growth | **+5,791 units — largest of any GB retail/leisure category** | Local Data Company |
| Net change 2023 | **+665 barbers** vs **−752 hairdressing salons** | LDC via British Beauty Council |
| Independents' share of barber units | **~99%** | LDC / Modern Barber |

Barbering is growing while hairdressing shrinks — the sector is splitting, and
the growing half is ours. Emerging multiples (Gould Barbers +45 units since
2019, Ted's Grooming Room) are still a rounding error against the independents.

### Market value — REPORT AS RANGES (house figure rule)

- **UK men's grooming products: £1.2bn–£2.3bn (2025)** depending on analyst —
  Data Bridge / Mobility Foresights at the low end, **Euromonitor at £2.3bn,
  +6% year on year**, at the top. Scope definitions differ; never print one
  figure as fact.
- **UK hair & beauty services turnover: £4.6bn–£6.1bn** — IBISWorld £5.8bn
  (2025), NHBF £6.1bn (2025/26), ResearchAndMarkets projecting only £4.6bn by
  2029. Up to £8.5bn on the widest "sector contribution" definitions.
- **Never blend the two lines**: products (Euromonitor) and services
  (IBISWorld/NHBF) are different markets. No analyst publishes a clean UK
  "barbering services" figure; IBISWorld sells a paywalled barbershop report if
  we ever need one.
- Global backdrop: men's grooming products **$298.9bn (2025) → $506.7bn by
  2033** (Grand View Research); US barbershop industry ~$7bn with BLS
  projecting 7% employment growth to 2033.

### Structure — who the reader actually is

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hair & beauty businesses | 50,400 | NHBF 2025/26 |
| People employed in the sector | 178,178 (NHBF) / ~220,000 widest | NHBF / PolicyBee |
| Hairdressers & barbers working in UK | ~121,500 | Statista 2024 |
| Self-employed share | **58% sector-wide; ~60–62% in hair/barbering** | NHBF / PolicyBee / ICAEW |
| Businesses with <5 staff | ~75% (95% under 10) | NHBF |
| Below the £90k VAT threshold | **93%** | NHBF |

The reader is an **owner-operator or chair-renter**, not a salon group. Chair
renting is the dominant self-employed model and HMRC issued new
employment-status guidance for hair and barbering in 2025 — the "disguised
employment" issue is live and NHBF campaigns on it constantly. This is our
Smart SME muscle: sub-£100k-turnover businesses run by their owners.

### Sector health — the honest half

- **NHBF State of the Sector, March 2026**: ~1 in 5 businesses at a loss;
  nearly three-quarters on razor-thin margins; 75% say recruitment is harder
  than a year ago; 30%+ unsure their business survives six months.
- Autumn 2024 Budget measures added an estimated **£139m in sector costs**
  (~£25k p.a. for a small business) → forecast 15% profit drop (NHBF,
  *Straightening Out the Costs*, Jan 2025). A record 74% had raised prices by
  April 2025. Typical men's cut now £15–£30; a £8–12 town cut in 2019 is
  £12–18 in 2026.
- **Apprenticeships are collapsing**: hairdressing starts down 70% in a decade
  (13,180 → 4,160); CBI Economics warns the pipeline could hit zero by 2027.
  Private barber academies are filling the gap commercially (London School of
  Barbering 4,000+ qualified, TotalBarber, Wahl's own academy, ~£2,500
  fast-track courses) — a growing advertiser segment born from the sector's
  biggest problem.
- Reputational backdrop: the NCA's **Operation Machinize** (2025–) targets
  money-laundering through cash-intensive barbershops — 900+ arrests, ~£10.7m
  seized. A real policy beat for us and a real editorial hazard (§9).

**Read:** footprint growing, margins squeezed, employment model fragmenting
into self-employment. That is not a weakness for a free trade title — it is
the *reason the title should exist*: JB's framing ("a business tool to make
anyone who runs this type of business make more profits") is exactly what a
margin-squeezed, 99%-independent sector needs and what nobody serves.

**The strategic shape:** the two live incumbents split the ground between them

title of any kind competes on search.** Contrast with fitness, where Health
Club Management sat at ~340k and owned the ground — there is no HCM here.

The counterweight: **PBG owns the venues.** Barber Connect (10,000+ attendees)
and Salon International's Modern Barber Stage are both theirs, so the biggest
UK advertiser gathering points belong to a competitor's parent. We compete for
budgets they meet at their own shows. Manageable — sponsor lists show brands
buy multiple channels — but worth knowing before Dec's equivalent conversation
happens here.

## 4. The contamination problem — worse than fitness

Fitness had "gym near me" (~100k/mo UK). Barbering has that *plus* a style-term
layer with no fitness equivalent:

| Consumer query | Volume | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| low taper fade | **550,000/mo** | global |
| modern mullet | 450,000/mo (+508% over 5yr) | global |
| butterfly haircut | 135,000/mo | global |
| french crop | 90,500/mo | global |
| "hair salons near me" | 3,350,000/mo | US |

"Barber near me" has overtaken branded searches in most UK cities (Queueaway,
2026). "Barber", "haircut", "fade", "clippers" (home users), "barber prices"
(consumers), "barber earnings" (career-switchers) are all contaminated stems.

**The engine must never be seeded with a bare style term or a bare "barber"
query.** Safely business-only stems, verified: chair rental/rent-a-chair,
barbershop insurance, barbershop fit-out, barber wholesale/trade supplies,
barbershop business plan / open a barbershop, barber booking software
comparisons, and the HMRC/VAT/rates compliance layer.

**JB's trend-content steer survives this — with a frame.** "Best hairstyles at
the World Cup" content is wanted and Modern Barber already proves the genre
(they ran exactly that piece in Aug 2026). But ours is always the *owner's*
version: what the trend means for your service menu, your prices, your booking
demand, how to sell the upgrade. The scope rule in §9 carries this.

## 5. Content supply — measured, not assumed

Observed cadence (dated feeds, 21 Aug 2026):

| Source | Usable cadence |
|---|---|
| BarberEVO UK/Ireland | ~0.5 UK items/week (10 items Mar–Aug 2026); 2–3/week site-wide |
| Modern Barber | ~3–4 items/week |
| NHBF policy/business updates | ~1 per week–fortnight |
| National-press crossover (VAT, Budget, crime crackdown) | episodic but real |
| Product launches reaching trade channels | a few per month |
| Trade shows/awards | 2–3 UK anchors/yr + a busy US circuit |

**Estimate: ~2–3 genuinely usable UK trade items/day; 4–5/day padded with
US/global and grooming-brand news lifted from the beauty press.** Same shape as
golf (~3/day against a 7/day target): the plan must be majority evergreen —
profitability guides, product/category explainers, marketing how-tos, shop
profiles, academy content — with the wire as seasoning, not the meal. Two
mitigations golf didn't have: a strong recurring policy beat (NHBF is an
active, quotable lobbying body) and an awards/competition circuit that
generates names and pictures year-round.

One caution the golf brief didn't need: **the news niche has incumbents**
(BarberEVO and Modern Barber both run news feeds). Our differentiation is not
being first to a press release — it is the business-first treatment nobody
gives it, and search, where neither of them exists.

## 6. Audience — the honest sizing

- **Core: ~18,400 barbershop owner-operators** (99% independent, so shops ≈
  decision-makers).
- **Extended: chair-renters and self-employed barbers** — at 58–62%
  self-employment across ~121,500 hairdressers/barbers, the barbering share
  plausibly puts another 20–40k self-employed operators in scope, each a
  micro-business making their own buying decisions (tools, products, booking
  app, insurance). This tail is the Smart SME playbook.
- **Plus:** academies and their students (the pipeline crisis makes training a
  growth segment), multiples' ops managers (small), and male-grooming brand/
  distributor staff (the advertisers themselves — trade readers who matter).

Honest read: the true UK business-decision-maker universe is roughly
**40–60k** — bigger than golf's, smaller than fleet's, and unusually
homogeneous (one trade, one set of problems), which makes both editorial and
ad sales simpler than either.

## 8. Design — the brief's explicit requirement

JB, verbatim intent: *"Visually, it needs to fit in with the look of the
industry, so be way less number/data looking than our current builds."*

This is the first title where the child theme is not just a re-skin. The
industry's aesthetic is image-led, dark, craft-heavy (look at BarberEVO,
Uppercut, Reuzel branding). Concretely:

- Photography-first article cards and hero layouts; the stats-and-tables
  furniture of Smart SME/Fleet must be optional per-title, not baked into
  `cogent-base` layouts.
- Likely a dark or high-contrast palette variant of the base theme.
- The picture pipeline matters *more* here than any previous title — barber
  content without strong imagery reads as fake to this audience. Budget for it.
- **Playbook implication:** scope what `cogent-base` needs so an image-led
  child is a supported variant rather than a fork. This is the launch's main
  engineering task, and it pays forward — the gym title (if it runs) wants the
  same.

## 9. Editorial risk and the standing rules

Two standing rules, analogous to fleet's figure rule and gym's claims rule:

1. **The owner-frame rule (scope).** We cover the *business* of barbering,
   never haircuts for consumers. Trend and style content is in scope only when
   framed for the shop: service menus, pricing, upsells, booking demand. The
   engine is never seeded with a bare style term or bare "barber" query
   (§4 list of safe stems). Sub-rule from the market data: **quote market
   figures as ranges with the analyst named** — products (£1.2–2.3bn) and
   services (£4.6–6.1bn) are different markets and must never be blended.
2. **The crime-coverage rule (libel).** The money-laundering crackdown
   (Operation Machinize, "Turkish barber" narrative) is a legitimate,
   recurring policy story — and a libel trap. We report the policy and the
   enforcement statistics; we **never** connect a named shop, chain or
   nationality-of-owner to the laundering narrative unless reporting a
   concluded prosecution from a primary source. The national press has been
   sloppy here; a trade title cannot be, because the accused are our readers.

Lesser hazards: product-claim inflation (grooming products make cosmetic
claims — attribute, don't assert; lighter than gym's health-claims problem)
and the recruitment/apprenticeship story, where the temptation to blame
(government, tax policy, the young) should be resisted in favour of NHBF data.
