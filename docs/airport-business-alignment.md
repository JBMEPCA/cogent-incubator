## Reader

Two tiers, both airport-side buyers (not airlines, not consumers):

1. **Senior decision makers** — CEO, MD, commercial director.
2. **Functional heads** — head of security, head of passenger experience, head
   of cargo.

They read to find out what other airports are doing and where to put time and
money next: legislation changes, ideas worth copying, products worth buying.
The winning frame is **"steal this idea / buy this thing"** — coverage the
reader can action.

**Not our reader:** holidaymakers. No consumer travel content, ever.

## Taste calibration

- **Favourite article archetype:** "Biometric Boarding System Airport Vendors
  Compared: Costs and Capabilities" — vendor comparisons with real costs;
  decision-support content that answers the buying question in black and white.
  Commission more of this format.
- **Exemplars (competitor):** Business Airport International's London City
  landing-approach consultation story (operational change other airports can
  learn from) and Signature's Palm Beach private terminal opening (guest
  experience angle). One operational, one experiential — cover both sides.
- **Never:** consumer travel advice, "best airports for your holiday",
  listicle-shaped generic AI content ("10 things you didn't know about
  airports").
- **The gap nobody owns:** personable content — interviews, commentary,
  operator case studies. Big competitors are weak here; it is also exactly the
  adjacency advertisers want. Interview pipeline is the differentiator.
