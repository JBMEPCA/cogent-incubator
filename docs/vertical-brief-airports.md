# Airports — vertical brief

Title #5 candidate, researched 24 Aug 2026. Tom's idea and Tom's notes drive the
scope. Same structure as fleet, gym/fitness, golf resorts and barbering: market,
competitors, demand, advertisers, risk, verdict. Sponsor: Tom.

## 0. Verdict

**Yes — with one strategic condition. The market, the money and the content supply
are the strongest of the five verticals we have assessed, but the incumbent trade
press is also the strongest we have ever faced. This title wins only if it enters
through the seam — the airport as a P&L, plus a structured global
expansion/tender/contract tracker — and never fights the ops-news incumbents
head-on.**

Four findings drive the yes.

1. **The money is on a different scale to anything we have done.** ACI puts the
   sector's capital need at **US$2.4 trillion by 2040**. Global airport revenue was
   **$194.9bn in 2024**, of which **$73.3bn is non-aeronautical** — the retail,
   parking, F&B and property money Tom's notes centre on — and 2026 is forecast as
   the first year world traffic passes **10 billion passengers**. Every expansion
   Tom listed checks out with live 2026 stories attached: Gatwick cleared its final
   court challenge on 4 Aug 2026, Western Sydney opens 25 Oct 2026, Dubai's $35bn
   DWC has >AED55bn of contracts to let by end-2026, Poland's CPK has a $10bn
   tender pipeline through 2026 alone.
2. **The commercial SERPs are undefended.** "How do airports make money",
   "non-aeronautical revenue airports", "cost to build an airport", "airport
   parking revenue", "airport advertising rates", "airport management software" —
   every one is held by vendors, consultancies, report mills, Quora, Medium or a
   lone Substack. **No trade publisher ranks for any of them.** The publishers that
   exist are ops-news and travel-retail titles that do not do money journalism.
3. **The wire supply is the best we have measured.** Scoped infrastructure queries
   (expansion, construction, investment, runway, baggage, tenders) across GB+US
   locales yield **3–5 usable B2B stories a day** — golf managed ~3 and fleet was
   similar. The engine will not go hungry.
4. **The advertiser universe is the deepest yet.** Roughly **1,900 exhibition
   stands were sold across six sector shows in the last twelve months** (PTE 400+,
   TFWA 482, inter airport Europe 377, Global Airports Forum ~300, Airspace World
   ~250, Airport Show Dubai 140+). One competitor (Airport Industry-News) has 60+
   suppliers paying for directory listings today — proof that a recurring paid
   directory works in this sector.

The three things that could kill it, in order:

- **The incumbent field is genuinely strong.** AviationPros (Tranco 102k), CAPA
  (112k), Airport Technology (135k), Moodie Davitt (153k), International Airport
  Review (244k). For calibration: we declined to attack Health Club Management at
  340k. The saving grace is that every one of these is strong *somewhere else* —
  none sits on our seam, and none defends the commercial SERPs above. But drift
  into generic ops news and we lose permanently.
- **No CIM advertiser crossover.** Golf launched into Dec's Hotel Magazine book.
  Airports launch cold — Hotel/Bar overlap (F&B fit-out, FM, washrooms) is
  marginal at best. The commercial validation has to come from the exhibitor
  lists, and from whatever Tom's own network turns out to be worth.
- **The obvious names are all encumbered.** AAAE publishes *Airport Magazine*, ACI
  Europe publishes *Airport Business*, the UK AOA publishes *The Airport
  Operator*. Naming needs the same trademark care as barbering (§10).

- Non-aero is ~37% of revenue and the high-margin share. ACI forecasts $76bn in

- Airport retail/duty free market size **diverges by scope**: $42.8bn (airport
  retail only, GlobalData) to $94.2bn (all travel retail, Mordor). House rule:
  quote the range and the firm, never one number (§9).
- Operator league in travel retail: Avolta ~$8.7bn revenue (+12.2%), then
  Lagardère, DFS/LVMH, King Power, Lotte.

### Pain points with citable numbers (the "what a bad day costs" beat)

- CrowdStrike outage, Jul 2024: Delta alone **$500–550m**, 5,000+ cancellations;
  ~$5.4bn Fortune 500 impact.
- Airport power demand ~**doubling by 2030**, 3.4–5x by 2040–50 (electrification);
  grid works >50% of electrification project cost.
- PFAS foam transition: ~$2.7m per US Part 139 airport; FAA $350m grant
  programme.
- ACI net zero 2050; 314 European airports have published net-zero roadmaps;
  ReFuelEU SAF mandate 2% (2025) → 70% (2050), SAF at ~340% of fossil kerosene.
- FAA controller staffing crisis (~13,460 FTE, chronic shortfalls) — disruption
  economics, covered as cost, not politics.

### Calibration — and the honest read

Fleet's incumbents were 2.5–2.8m and we beat them. Golf's clustered 458k–2.5m.
Health Club Management sat at 340k and we called it too strong to attack. **The
airport trade field runs 102k–645k, with five titles stronger than HCM.** On raw
numbers this is a bottom-of-market entry, full stop.

What makes it viable anyway is that the strength is all somewhere else. Map the
strong five by what they actually defend: AviationPros = US ground support; CAPA =
paywalled airline analysis; Airport Technology = lead-gen listicles; Moodie =
brand-side travel retail; IAR = ops/security/tech news. **Nobody publishes free,
web-first, operator-facing journalism on how airports make money, and nobody runs
a structured global expansion/tender/contract tracker.** GlobalData sells contract
data at $995+/report; Momberger sells it as a paid email newsletter with no
website; Airport Improvement does it well but US-only. The ACI organs — the
"official" incumbents — are print relics (577k and not-in-top-1m).

That is the seam, and it is exactly Tom's brief: **the airport as a P&L
(non-aero revenue, cost, disruption economics) + the expansion/procurement
record (who's building, what it costs, what's been awarded, what's late).**
Ops-news head-terms belong to IAR and Airport Technology; we do not contest them.

## 4. The contamination problem

Measured, not assumed. The raw `airport` Google News feed is **~15–20% usable**
— the rest is security breaches, weather chaos, strikes, crime, viral videos and
flight-delay listicles. "Airport terminal" runs ~30% usable, "airport parking"
~15% (fines, scams, discount codes). The consumer opposition sits at Tranco
2k–16k — stronger than golf's consumer set.

But the shape is more manageable than golf's, because the contamination lives in
*generic* queries, not the core beat: "airport construction" is ~85% usable,
"airport investment" ~85%, "baggage handling system" ~85%, "airport expansion"
~75%. Scope the seeds and the feed is clean.

**Rules:**

> **The buyer rule (airport edition).** Every article is written for someone who
> is *paid* to be at an airport — operator, director, terminal/ops manager,
> commercial director, developer, contractor, supplier. If the natural reader is
> a passenger, it is not our article. No travel tips, no lounge reviews, no
> passenger-rights content, no flight-delay news, no "best airports to sleep in".
> Disruption is covered as a cost line, not as travel news.

> **Never seed the engine with the bare queries `airport`, `airport terminal` or
> `airport parking`.** Scoped queries only, per §5.

## 6. Audience — the honest sizing

The property count is small and institutional: **811 ACI members operating
~2,200 airports; ~2,800 commercial airports worldwide.** Two very different
buying structures:

- **~20 ownership groups control several hundred airports** with centralised
  procurement: Aena (46 + Luton + 17 Brazil; world's largest at 384.8m pax),
  VINCI (70+, incl. Gatwick/Edinburgh), Groupe ADP (~26 + TAV 46% + GMR 49%),
  Fraport (~30), Corporación América (~52), AviAlliance (bought AGS for £1.53bn,
  Jan 2025), Adani (8), GMR, TAV (~15), Schiphol, Avinor (43), Swedavia (10),
  AOT, Changi, Incheon, MAG, Heathrow.
- **~450 US commercial airports are independent city/county/authority buyers**
  making decisions by public RFP — which is why US titles survive on advertising
  and why AAAE claims its events reach 15,000+ decision-makers a year.

As with golf, **the addressable audience is the supply chain, not just the
properties**: PTE alone drew 12,500+ attendees; TFWA 7,999; Airspace World
7,000+; Airport Show Dubai 7,000+; ACI-NA 2,000+ professionals from 200+
airports; FTE Global 2,000+ executives. Add the architects, engineers,
contractors and consultants delivering the §1 pipeline.

**Reader definition to lock before launch:** airport C-suite · terminal/ops
manager · commercial/non-aero director · development & planning lead ·
procurement · route development · ownership-group HQ · investor/lender ·
architect/engineer/contractor · supplier sales & BD.

## 8. Tools we can build

Ranked by "creates a proprietary, citable number". All reduce to the same
primitive as golf's: **a structured project/property database with scheduled
enrichment.** Build it once.

1. **Global Airport Development Tracker (build first — this is the flagship).**
   Every expansion project: cost, phase, status, contractors, architects, tenders
   open, contracts awarded, delays. The competition is a $995 GlobalData report
   and a 1973-vintage paid email newsletter. A free, live, filterable version
   becomes the reference for every "airport development in X" query, feeds the
   news desk (every status change is a story), and is the natural landing page
   for every contractor and consultant backlink. §1 is the seed data.
2. **Non-Aero Revenue League.** Annual league table of commercial revenue per
   passenger, parking yield, retail spend per pax — built from listed operators'
   accounts (Aena, ADP, Fraport, VINCI, AOT, the Mexican groups all publish) plus
   ACI aggregates. Nobody publishes this accessibly; it is the citable heart of
   the P&L beat and a guaranteed annual franchise.
3. **Contract-award ledger.** Who won what, when, for how much — feeds tool 1
   and makes "who's building it" queries ours.
4. **Drop-off/parking charge index.** Scrape published kerb-side and parking
   charges across a fixed global basket, publish the movements. The UK press
   reruns the RAC's UK version every year; a global monthly index is cheap,
   automatable link bait squarely on the parking-revenue beat.
5. **Disruption cost ledger.** Outages, closures, strikes, snow days — with the
   published cost of each. Directly answers Tom's "what an IT outage or a snow
   day actually costs" and compounds into a citable dataset.
6. **Games-ready tracker.** The event-driven pipeline (LA28 → WC2030 → Brisbane
   2032 → Saudi 2034) as a standing franchise page with countdown status per
   host airport. High search interest, refreshes itself.

## 9. Editorial risk

Four rules, two inherited and two new.

1. **The buyer rule** (§4). Load-bearing, as in golf.
2. **The scope-and-figure rule.** Airport numbers diverge by *scope*, not just by
   firm: duty free is $42.8bn or $94bn depending on the definition; Riyadh is
   $30bn, $50bn or $100bn depending on the boundary; CPK's $43bn includes
   railways; Morocco grew from $2.8bn to $4.2bn. Every figure carries source,
   year and scope, or a range. Never one bare number in our own voice.
3. **The geopolitics rule.** Saudi giga-projects, Routes World's move from Riyadh
   to Frankfurt over regional tensions, Nuctech's exclusion from Western
   procurement, TSA politics, Heathrow's national-policy fight. **Business
   stories only: capital, capacity, contracts, costs.** No editorial line on
   human rights, trade policy or domestic politics — same standard as golf.
4. **The incident rule (new, airport-specific).** Crashes, security breaches and
   evacuations are the tabloid layer of this sector and the fastest route to both
   consumer drift and reputational damage for an AI-written title. We do not
   cover safety incidents or security breaches as news. Disruption enters our
   pages only as economics (cost, resilience spend, procurement consequence) —
   and security coverage stays at procurement level (who bought which scanner),
   never at vulnerability level (how screening fails).

No YMYL exposure. Sustainability is covered as Tom framed it — cost reduction
and compliance economics — which conveniently is also the defensible framing.

### Visual requirement (Tom's, and it is a build requirement)

Serious and technical: big architectural photography, dense data in real tables
and charts. **Closer to the current builds — the opposite pole to barbering.**
The base-theme child needs a data-forward variant, not a softened one; the §8
tools give the site its data spine.
