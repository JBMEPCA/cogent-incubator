Decision one: how we pay for the newsletter

At 15,000 subscribers a title we would hold 450,000 contacts across 30 titles. Mailchimp and most of the market bill on contacts held; Amazon SES and equivalents bill on emails sent. Same newsletter, same subscribers, wildly different invoice.

Fleet Per contact Per send Difference / yr
10 titles £1,289 £481 £9,700
20 titles £2,483 £925 £18,700
30 titles £3,586 £1,255 £28,000

Recommendation: build sending in-house on SES. Two to three weeks of work against a £28,000 a year saving at target scale. The trade is that deliverability becomes ours to manage — bounce handling, complaint rates, IP warming — which needs doing properly or the newsletter stops arriving.

Decision two: add titles in batches, not one at a time

Hosting and the database both step up a tier at 5 titles and again at 15. The step costs the same whether one title crosses it or ten do — so a badly-timed single launch costs three to four times what it should.

Adding title 5, 7, 15, 17 … 30 £33 a month each
Adding the 6th title £96 a month
Adding the 16th title £148 a month

Practical rule: hold at 5, then move to 10–15 in one push. Hold at 15, then move to 20+ in one push.

What we need agreed

1. A monthly ceiling. £500 buys about 10 titles, £1,000 about 22, £1,500 about 35 — on send-priced email. On contact-priced email the same budgets buy 3, 8 and 12.

2. Sign-off to build sending in-house. Two to three weeks of engineering, and it should happen before fleet-wide subscribers pass roughly 50,000 — after that, every month of delay costs more than the work does.

3. The first batch of titles. Sectors drive everything downstream — the research sources, the section taxonomy, and how defensible the portfolio looks to Google.

One caveat. These are modelled from how the Smart SME engine behaves in code, not from measured invoices. There is a known bug in its own cost reporting that may be inflating cheap-model spend fivefold, so the first task is to fix that and replace these estimates with 30 days of real data. The shape of the argument will not change; the second decimal place might.

Baseline: optimised engine, one article per title per day, 15,000 subscribers per title, send-priced email, £1 = $1.27. An interactive version of this model — where fleet size, publishing rate, subscriber count and both decisions above can be varied — is available on request.
