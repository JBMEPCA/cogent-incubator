# Dental practices (UK) - vertical brief

Wave candidate, researched 18 Sep 2026. Same structure as the airports and barbering
briefs: market, competitors, demand, contamination, supply, audience, advertisers,
risk, naming, verdict. Scope as briefed: a business title for dental practice owners
(principals) and practice managers, never clinicians as clinicians. NHS contract and
UDAs, private and plan conversion, practice sales, valuations and group
consolidation, staffing, CQC, patient acquisition, finance, software, equipment,
fit-outs, and aesthetics as a business line.

## 0. Verdict

**Conditional, and the weakest of the wave on the one dimension that has decided
every launch so far: the seam is not unattended. The money is excellent and the
advertisers are proven, but FMC (Dentistry.co.uk, Tranco 206,842) already publishes
a Business news section, runs the ADG Conference ("the business of dentistry"), the
DSO Awards and the Private Dentistry Awards, and ranks in the business SERPs we would
target. Proceed only on a narrower seam (the practice as an asset: deals, valuations,
group consolidation and NHS contract economics, driven by trackers and data) and only
with one named advertiser in hand. Score: 56/100 (§11).**

Four findings drive that.

1. **The money is real and moving.** LaingBuisson values UK high-street dentistry at
   **£12.16bn (2023/24, +9.2%)**, of which **£8.4bn (69%) is private pay**, the highest
   private share since it began recording in 2007. Christie & Co forecasts about
   **£15bn by 2027/28**. Average net profit per principal is now **£193k to £198k**
   across NHS, mixed and private (NASDAL 2025). Bridgepoint bought mydentist in Oct
   2025 at an implied **£750m to £800m** *(Dental Group Signal estimate, unverified)*.
   Practices trade at **6.5x to 9.5x EBITDA** *(broker blog figure, unverified)* and
   57% of accepted offers in H1 2026 were at or above asking price (Christie & Co).
2. **The advertisers are already spending, visibly.** About **1,000 exhibitor stands a
   year** across four UK shows (BDCDS Birmingham 400+, BDIA Dental Showcase 300+,
   Dentistry Show London 180+, Scottish Dental Show 130+). Display ads live on the
   incumbents today: Straumann, Belmont, Invisalign, Planmeca, Carestream, Solventum,
   W&H, Acteon, Listerine, Dentists Provident. This is the strongest advertiser proof
   of any UK vertical we have assessed.
3. **But the business seam has an owner.** Unlike barbering (no ranked title) or
   airports (strong titles, none on our seam), dentistry's strongest trade site is
   also its most business-literate. FMC partners with the ADG (dental groups) and ADAM
   (practice managers), sells practice services (HR, compliance, marketing), and is
   effectively the only UK dental trade title Google News indexes. Two indie
   newsletters already sit on the exact seam (Dental Group Signal on Substack; Dental
   Practice Owner on beehiiv).
4. **The business SERPs are held by unranked minnows, not publishers.** Brokers
   (Samera, Dental Elite, PFM), accountants (DJH), solicitors, plan providers and a
   wave of AI-written vendor blogs (udapulse, dentalfinancepartners, updent,
   dominatedental) hold most slots; none is in Tranco's top 1m. Dentistry.co.uk is the
   only publisher appearing, in about a third of the queries tested. Winnable on
   domain strength, but not uncontested.

The three things that could kill it, in order:

- **FMC.** A web-strong incumbent that already covers the business of dentistry,
  owns the group-leadership event, and partners with both the groups' trade body and
  the managers' association. We would be fighting it for the same advertisers at its
  own shows.
- **No CIM asset.** JB confirmed 18 Sep 2026 that CIM has no legacy dental title. The
  internal-asset row that carried golf (Hotel/Bar) and barbering (DOG Grooming) is
  empty. Launch is cold.
- **Contamination plus health adjacency.** Patient demand swamps the vertical
  ("dentist near me" 165k+/mo, "emergency dentist" 90k+/mo) and the sector is
  health-regulated (GDC, CQC, advertising rules, aesthetics prescription-only
  medicines). Manageable with the scope and claims rules, but heavier than any title
  except gym.

**Read:** a £12bn market shifting from NHS to private at the fastest rate on record,
with profits recovering and PE-backed consolidators buying. Every one of those
movements is a business story, and most of them happen to independent owners making
five- and six-figure decisions (conversion, sale, fit-out, finance).

### The NHS contract beat (the recurring policy engine)

- **April 2026 changes in England** (not full reform): urgent care now a flat £75 per
  course (£15 per mandated slot plus £60 on delivery), minimum unscheduled care at
  8.2% of contract value for contracts of 100+ UDAs, re-banded fissure sealants and
  denture work, three complex-care pathways with national tariffs. UDA values still
  vary by contract, roughly £15 to £40+, minimum £28 under the recovery plan; patient
  charges Band 1 £27.90, Band 2 £76.60, Band 3 £332.10 *(secondary sources)*.
- **Live fight, this week:** the BDA (17 Sep 2026) rejected the ADG's call for
  incremental reform as it met the new dentistry minister, James Frith. BDA survey:
  1% of dentists expect to increase NHS work after the April changes, 63% expect to
  reduce it.
- Devolved nations run separate contracts: Wales contract reform, the Northern Ireland
  GDS Cost of Service Review (published 16 Sep 2026), Scotland's fee reform. Four
  contract regimes means four recurring policy threads.

| Bupa Dental Care | ~490 clinics UK and ROI | Only major group not PE-owned |

The consolidation story is the most business-first material in the sector, and it is

### Workforce (the pain point with numbers)

- GDC register Dec 2025: **131,680 registrants; 47,916 dentists (+3.4%); 83,764 DCPs**,
  including 68,472 dental nurses, 11,292 hygienists, 8,661 therapists (+21%).
- **53% of new dentist registrants in 2025 were internationally qualified**, the first
  year ever above half.
- Dental nurse sponsorship visas collapsed after the 22 Jul 2025 skilled-worker change:
  **6 grants in Q4 2025 to Q2 2026 vs 122 in the prior three quarters (-95%)**.
- Vacancy rates: 19% NHS, 15% private (Christie & Co survey).

## 4. The contamination problem

Measured and sourced where possible. Third-party UK volumes (Whito, citing Google
Keyword Planner, Q1 2026) *(unverified; tool figures vary, one source gives 246k for
"dentist near me")*:

| Consumer query | UK monthly searches |
|---|---|
| dentist near me | **165,000+** |
| emergency dentist | 90,000+ |
| NHS dentist accepting patients | 74,000+ |
| teeth whitening [city] | 40,000+ |
| dental implants [city] | 33,000+ |
| Invisalign [city] | 27,000+ |

Add the treatment-cost layer ("veneers cost UK", "composite bonding cost", "Turkey
teeth") with no measured volume but hundreds of practice and farm pages competing.
**Patient intent outnumbers owner intent by two to three orders of magnitude.**

News feed contamination, measured 18 Sep 2026 (§5): the bare `dentist` feed is
roughly **35% usable** and most of the usable items are FMC's own articles; the rest is
consumer health advice, obituaries, crime, celebrity smiles, Indian NEET admissions
and foreign stories. `dental practice` runs about 45% usable. Group names collide
badly: a query including "Riverdale" (a UK dental group) returned 100 items of which
2 were dental (the TV series, US schools). "Private" pulls in NEET counselling and
US obituaries.

**Rules:**

> **The owner rule (dental edition).** Every article is written for someone who runs,
> owns, finances, buys, sells, supplies or manages a practice. If the natural reader
> is a patient, it is not our article. No "how to find an NHS dentist", no treatment
> price guides for patients, no oral-health advice, no "is composite bonding worth
> it". Access-crisis stories enter only as contract economics and capacity.

> **Never seed the engine with bare `dentist`, `dental`, `teeth`, `NHS dentist`,
> `teeth whitening`, or a bare group name.** Group names must be paired with a
> dental term and a deal verb (acquires, practices, portfolio).

## 6. Audience, the honest sizing

- **Practices: 12,052.** About 8,000 are independent single or dual-site, so roughly
  8,000 independent owner decision units plus ~2,300 group-owned sites.
- **Principals/owners:** many practices have two or more partners; a plausible range
  is **10,000 to 14,000 owners** *(estimate, unverified; no published count)*.
- **Practice managers:** roughly one per practice, so **~11,000 to 12,000** *(estimate,
  unverified)*. ADAM, the only managers' association, claimed 600+ member practices
  (2020) and is partnered with FMC and BDIA.
- **Group HQ and DSO executives:** a few hundred senior buyers across ~20 groups;
  the ADG Conference is their event.
- **Wider:** 47,916 dentists (many associates who want to become owners), 131,680
  total registrants, plus brokers, accountants, lenders and supplier sales teams.

Honest read: a UK business-decision universe of roughly **20,000 to 30,000 people**,
highly homogeneous and high-income (average principal profit ~£196k). Smaller than
barbering's 40k to 60k and UK-only (Ireland is a small add-on), but each reader is worth
far more to an advertiser than a barbershop owner.

**Reader definition to lock before launch:** principal/owner · practice manager ·
group operations and M&A · associate planning ownership · practice-sale adviser ·
lender/accountant · supplier sales and BD.

## 9. Editorial risk

Four rules. Clinical/YMYL exposure is real but lower than gym, because the reader is
an owner, not a patient.

1. **The owner rule** (§4). Load-bearing.
2. **The scope rule (clinical).** We cover the business of a practice, never clinical
   technique, treatment choice, or oral-health advice. No "how to place an implant",
   no "best composite", no treatment outcome claims. The incumbents' clinical CPD
   layer is theirs; we do not enter it. Product launches are covered as commercial
   news (price, distribution, market), not as clinical recommendations.
3. **The claims rule.** Any health or treatment claim is attributed to a named source,
   never asserted in our voice. Extra care on aesthetics as a business line: botulinum
   toxin is a prescription-only medicine, tooth whitening above 0.1% hydrogen peroxide
   is restricted to registered dental professionals, and England's licensing scheme for
   non-surgical cosmetics is in train *(current status unverified)*. We cover the
   revenue, regulation and liability of adding aesthetics, never how-to.
4. **The fitness-to-practise rule (libel).** GDC hearings, CQC enforcement and
   "illegal pop-up whitening" stories are legitimate regulatory news and a libel trap,
   because the accused are our readers. Report policy, statistics and concluded
   outcomes from primary sources only; never name an individual from a complaint,
   investigation or unconcluded hearing.

**Figure rule** carries over from barbering and airports: market values differ by
scope (LaingBuisson £12.16bn high-street, IBISWorld and others differ); always name
the source and year, and never blend NHS budget, market value and practice income.

**Collision with our own titles:** low. Smart SME may have touched dental-practice
finance stories; import any such URLs into the keyword registry before launch.

## 12. Before committing

1. **Decide whether this belongs in the wave at all.** On this evidence it ranks below
   a title with an open seam or a CIM crossover. If the wave needs one fewer title,
   this is the one to drop.
2. **If proceeding: one named advertiser first.** There is no Dec or DOG Grooming book
   to lean on. Target the owner-buying layer (a broker, a plan provider, a software
   vendor, or a lender) for a launch sponsorship before any build.
3. **Commit to the narrow seam**, not "business of dentistry" in general: deals and
   groups tracker, NHS contract data, openings/closures, private conversion. Generic
   practice-management explainers put us head-on with FMC and the AI farms.
4. **Register dentalbusinessmagazine.co.uk and .com** only once 1 to 3 are decided
   (both free today; no sign anyone is circling).
5. **Ahrefs month**: size the business queries in §3 and split owner vs patient intent.
6. **Engine seeding**: scoped queries only (§4 rule); add a `DENTAL_NEWS_SEARCHES`
   block with NHS contract, devolved contracts, workforce/visa, CQC/GDC regulation,
   openings, UK group deals; plus a filter to down-weight dentistry.co.uk as a source
   so the wire does not become a rewrite of the incumbent.
