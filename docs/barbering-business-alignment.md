# Barbering Business — title alignment (from Tom's sheet, 29 Aug 2026)

Third sheet returned, and the first from someone sector-close: Tom ran Barber
Magazine. Where this sheet and a general business instinct differ, the sheet
wins, and it differs in one big way: the tone the engine had been running was
a business magazine's, and the reader is not a business magazine reader.

**Rolled out 6 Sep 2026** by `scripts/apply-alignment.mjs`. The brief text
lives in `scripts/alignment/barbering-business.*` and is what the Site row,
the seed script and this doc all read.

## Reader

Anyone with spending power in a barbershop: the owner mostly, the manager in
a bigger shop or chain, and the chair-renting self-employed barber who buys
their own tools, capes, razors, clippers, booking software, card machine and
accountant. They know barbering and not business: profitability, running more
efficiently, cutting costs, filling more appointments, extra revenue streams,
marketing themselves.

**Not our reader:** consumers reading barbering as culture, people with hair
loss, ladies' salon owners.

## What changed in the brief

- **The so-what rule.** Every business point spelled out to its end, in Tom's
  own example: deposits mean fewer no-shows, which means less dead time, which
  means more per hour, which means more profit. Obvious to a business owner,
  not to a barber, and never condescending.
- **The positive-turn rule.** No negative story without the fix in the same
  piece. Politics is out.
- **The consumer-trend rule**, restated Tom's way: "this trend is in, so offer
  it and bookings rise", never "best styles this summer".
- **The general-economy rule.** BCC, inflation, NEET and business-rates stories
  run only when the barbershop consequence is the headline. Several published
  pieces failed that test.
- **The policy-piece balance.** Tom's least favourite is the HMRC electronic
  sales suppression piece: useful, ranks in search, never clicked on the site
  against something visual. Policy pieces capped at roughly one in four, each
  with a "what to do" headline. News section target cut from 8 to 5; Trends
  and Services 5 to 7; Products and Tools 6 to 7; Marketing and Clients 5 to 6.
- **The monthly kit round-up** as a standing Products and Tools format: the
  coverage the small styling brands want to sit beside.
- **House style rewritten** to a younger, plainer voice: "someone like them",
  second person, no business-school words, no hand-wringing. The no-puns rule
  stays: youthful is not jokey.

## Commercial

Seen advertising "everywhere": Wahl, Andis, Uppercut Deluxe, Dojo, Captain
Fawcett, Slick Gorilla, Barber Blades. Tom's old Barber Mag sales ledger is
the real list: **JB to get it from Tom.**

Wishlist, in Tom's tiers rather than names: early money is the small styling
product brands (cream, paste, wax) and the shop-tech start-ups (websites,
marketing for shops); long term, electrical tools (clippers, dryers), booking
systems (Booksy and rivals), furniture (Takara Belmont), payments (Dojo, Tyl
by NatWest). Eighteen advertiser prospects seeded from that.

Adjacency they want: tips that show why a product category pays back, with
the brands named. Never "our product is amazing".

Tom's commercial read, worth keeping: the sector grew from £5 trims to £20-plus
with extras and the brands did not grow with it. The old magazine failed
because the big brands (Wahl, Andis, Uppercut, L'Oréal, Gillette) would not
come in and the small ones were bad payers. This engine's costs are low enough
to take small budgets, and nobody gets content until they have paid.

## Competitors and the gap

Modern Barber (Professional Beauty Group, linked to HJ) is the one that
matters. Tom's read: smoke and mirrors, arrogant, beatable on socials. Lead
sourcing can look sideways at ladies' hairdressing and pet grooming (clipper
and scissor brands sell to both).

## The vision, in Tom's words

Worst case: regular small bookings of £60 to £500. Best case: takes off on
socials, £10-15k a month, an app and small events. Most likely: lots of small
regular bookings and a few year-long campaigns.
