# Retirement villages: vertical brief

Title candidate, researched 18 Sep 2026. Same structure as fleet, gym/fitness,
golf resorts, barbering and airports: market, competitors, demand, advertisers,
risk, verdict. Scope: integrated retirement communities (IRCs), retirement
villages, housing-with-care, extra care, assisted living and the later-living
developers, operators, investors and suppliers behind them. No sponsor named yet.

Adjacent CIM asset: **Care Home Magazine** (carehomemagazine.co.uk, CIM Online
Ltd, Faversham). It is both the best advertiser crossover this title has and the
biggest collision risk. §9 draws the line.

## 0. Verdict

**Conditional yes, as a UK-led title with an Australia/New Zealand second
engine, and never head-on in the US. The UK has no free, web-first trade title
for retirement communities at all, which is the cleanest unattended seam we have
found; but the UK reader universe is small, the UK wire alone is thin (about 1.5
usable stories a day) and the whole thing only works if the line with Care Home
Magazine is drawn before launch and held.**

Scored total: **61/100** (§11). Below airports and golf, above anything we
declined.

Four findings drive the yes.

1. **The UK seam is genuinely empty.** Coverage today is scattered across
   paywalled generalists: Property Week (Tranco 155,650) and Estates Gazette
   (353,982) cover the deals, Inside Housing (137,327) covers the social-housing
   end, Housing LIN (not in top 1m) is a knowledge network, Care Home
   Professional (978,089) covers care homes with the odd village story.
   **Nobody publishes free, web-first journalism for the people who develop,
   fund and run retirement communities.** The US has three (Senior Housing
   News, McKnight's Senior Living, Seniors Housing Business); Australia has The
   Weekly Source (paywalled). The UK has none.
2. **The money is arriving and the rules are being rewritten.** ARCO projects
   £40bn of investment and £70bn turnover by 2030. Audley and Elysian merged in
   July 2025 into a group with over £3bn of sales value. Legal & General
   (Inspired Villages), AXA (Retirement Villages Group), Goldman Sachs
   (Riverstone), Octopus and Schroders are all in. Event fees now have a legal
   definition, a revised NPPF (Aug 2026) tells councils to plan for older
   people's housing, and New Zealand announced a nine-month repayment cap on 17-18
   Sep 2026. Regulation is a live, recurring business story.
3. **The business-intent SERPs have no publisher in them.** "C2 or C3 use class
   retirement", "how do retirement villages make money", "cost to build a
   retirement village", "retirement community sales and marketing" are held by
   law firms, NZ/AU financial advisers, content farms and marketing agencies.
   Every one of those is a prospective advertiser.
4. **CIM already owns half the supplier book.** Care Home Magazine's supplier
   directory lists **350+ companies** (catering, laundry, furniture, nurse call,
   care software, training). IRCs buy most of those categories, plus the
   hospitality-grade interiors and F&B that The Hotel Magazine already sells to.

The three things that could kill it, in order:

- **Cannibalising Care Home Magazine.** Extra care, assisted living and "care
  home plus apartments" schemes sit on the border. Without a hard scope rule
  (§9) the AI engine will drift into care-home news, compete for CHM's keywords
  and confuse CHM's advertisers.
- **Consumer contamination.** "Retirement villages near me", "deferred
  management fee explained" and "is a retirement village worth it" are huge,
  consumer-led and dominated by Lottie, carehome.co.uk, Which? and the operators
  themselves. The engine will write for residents unless it is told not to.
- **A small UK reader universe and a thin UK wire.** ARCO has 29 operator
  members running ~250 communities; the IRC sector is 70,000 units. The title
  needs the investors, planners, architects, councils and AU/NZ to reach
  critical mass, and needs the AU/NZ wire to hit five a day.

### Calibration and the honest read

Fleet's incumbents were 2.5-2.8m and we beat them. Golf's clustered 458k-2.5m.
Airports ran 102k-645k and we went in only through a seam. Health Club
Management at 340k was called too strong.

**In the UK there is no dedicated incumbent to rank.** The only publishers above
HCM strength (Inside Housing, Property Week) are generalists behind paywalls
whose later-living coverage is a section, not a product. The dedicated bodies
(ARCO, Housing LIN) are not publishers and not in the top 1m. **The US is the
opposite:** SHN, McKnight's and Seniors Housing Business own every US operator
query, and US wire volume is theirs by default. Going global head-on means
fighting SHN on its own ground. We do not.

**The seam: the UK retirement community as a business (development, capital,
planning, operator economics, sales and regulation), benchmarked against the
mature AU/NZ markets that share its fee model.** US coverage enters only as
capital markets (REIT and platform deals that move UK money) and as benchmarks.

## 4. The contamination problem

Measured, not assumed. The raw `"retirement village"` Google News feed (44
items in 7 days) is **~45% usable business news**, but that figure is flattered
by the NZ reform week; strip that and it falls to ~30%. The rest is ballroom
dancing, residents turning 113, robberies at South African villages, awards and
"is retirement living worth it" pieces aimed at families. `"senior living"`
without a scope term pulls obituaries and Indian consumer property.
`"later living"` pulls film sets and a pool project. `"extra care" housing`
is ~25% usable (the phrase matches generic "extra care").

Two contamination vectors are specific to this sector and matter more than in
airports:

1. **Resident-complaint journalism.** Much of the real regulatory news (NZ
   repayment waits, AU fee payouts, UK event fees) is written from the
   resident's side. It is legitimate business news, but an AI engine rewriting
   it will drift into consumer advice.
2. **Care-home news.** "Care home" is the highest-volume adjacent term (100+
   items/7 days, capped by the feed). It belongs to Care Home Magazine (§9).

**Rules:**

> **The operator rule.** Every article is written for someone who is *paid* to
> develop, fund, run, design, supply or regulate a retirement community:
> operator, developer, investor, lender, planner, architect, council commissioner,
> supplier. If the natural reader is a resident or their family, it is not our
> article. No "how to choose a retirement village", no "is it worth it", no
> fee explainers written for buyers. Resident complaints are covered as
> regulatory and reputational risk to operators, never as advice to residents.

> **Never seed the engine with the bare queries `retirement village`,
> `senior living`, `later living`, `extra care` or `care home`.** Scoped
> queries only, per §5.

## 6. Audience: the honest sizing

The operator count is small. Be honest about it.

- **UK IRC operators:** ARCO has **29 member organisations** (incl.
  provisional) running **~250 communities**, which ARCO says is about half the
  sector. So perhaps 50-70 IRC operators in total *(estimate)*.
- **UK retirement housing providers** (440,000 units, no onsite care): McCarthy
  & Stone, Churchill, Anchor, Housing 21, Lifestory, Beechcroft, plus housing
  associations with older-people's stock. A few hundred organisations
  *(estimate)*.
- **Local authorities:** 153 English councils with adult social care duties
  commission extra care, plus Welsh, Scottish and NI bodies, plus every local
  planning authority deciding C2/C3 applications.
- **Capital:** insurers, pension funds, PE, banks and specialist lenders (L&G,
  AXA, Goldman, Octopus, Schroders, Paragon, OakNorth, REL).
- **Advisers:** the 80 firms in ARCO's supplier directory alone (law, agents,
  architects, cost consultants), plus Knight Frank, Savills, CBRE, JLL, Carterwood.
- **Event reach, as the best proxy:** ARCO What Next? **330+ delegates from 160+
  organisations**; Retirement Living & Care UK **350+**; Housing LIN 2026
  **~400 across two cities**; Property Week Later Living & Care "hundreds".
  These overlap heavily.

**Honest UK core: roughly 3,000-6,000 decision-makers** *(estimate from event
and membership proxies; unverified)*. That is smaller than fleet or Smart SME
by an order of magnitude and comparable to golf's owner layer. **AU/NZ roughly
doubles it:** Australia has 2,300+ villages (2014 count, stale), NZ's RVA
conference draws ~700 delegates and ~250 exhibitors *(unverified, secondary
source)*.

**Reader definition to lock before launch:** operator CEO/COO · development and
land director · sales and marketing director · village/general manager ·
investor/fund manager · lender · planner (public and private) · council
commissioner · architect/designer · adviser (legal, agent, cost) · supplier
sales and BD.

## 8. Tools we can build

All reduce to the same primitive as golf and airports: **a structured
scheme/operator database with scheduled enrichment.** Build it once.

1. **UK Retirement Community Pipeline Tracker (flagship).** Every IRC and
   extra care scheme: operator, units, tenure (sale/rent/shared ownership),
   use class claimed (C2/C3), planning status and appeal outcomes, funder,
   architect, contractor. Knight Frank and Carterwood hold this privately; nobody
   publishes it free. Every planning decision is a story; every scheme page is a
   backlink target for the architect, planner and lawyer involved.
2. **C2/C3 Appeal Ledger.** Every appeal decision on use class, CIL and
   affordable housing for older people's schemes. Answers the one question
   every developer and planning lawyer asks, feeds the law-firm SERP (§3) and
   gives law firms a reason to cite us.
3. **Operator Economics Benchmark (UK vs AU vs NZ).** Entry prices, DMF/event
   fee structures, service charges, occupancy and resale times, built from
   listed-operator accounts (Ryman, Summerset, Oceania, Stockland, Aveo) and UK
   published fee pages. The international comparison the UK sector keeps asking
   for, and the natural home for the event-fee regulation story.
4. **Capital Tracker.** Every platform deal, JV, fund raise and development
   facility (e.g. Beechcroft/Paragon £44.8m) with lender, size and yield where

## 9. Editorial risk and the Care Home Magazine line

### The scope line with Care Home Magazine (lock before launch)

> **The front-door rule.** If the resident has their own front door and holds
> a lease, licence or tenancy, it is ours. If the resident has a room and a
> care plan in a CQC-registered care home, it is Care Home Magazine's.

| Topic | New title | Care Home Magazine |
|---|---|---|
| Retirement villages, IRCs, extra care, assisted living, retirement housing | **Yes** | No |
| Care homes (residential, nursing, dementia homes) | No | **Yes** |
| Care home development, planning, deals (e.g. Muller, Vistry care home sites) | No | **Yes** (CHM already runs these) |
| Mixed campus (village + care home) | Development, capital, village operations | The care home's operation, CQC ratings, staffing |
| Domiciliary care delivered inside an IRC | Only as operating model and cost line | CQC regulation, clinical practice, care workforce |
| Care workforce, pay, strikes, CQC inspections | No | **Yes** |
| Catering, interiors, tech, wellbeing | Villages' purchasing lens | Care homes' purchasing lens; same advertisers, cross-sold |
| Event fees, DMFs, C2/C3, land, sales, investment | **Yes** | No |

**Keyword rule:** the new title never targets any query containing `care home`,
`nursing home` or `residential care`; Care Home Magazine never targets
`retirement village`, `retirement community`, `later living` or `extra care`.
Both go into the keyword registry (see the Fleet collision lesson) and the
outreach exclusion list before the first article. The wire must never ingest
the `"care home"` feed.

**Commercial rule:** sold as a pair, never against each other. One CIM
"later life" package across both titles for the 350+ shared suppliers.

### Other rules

1. **The operator rule** (§4). Load-bearing.
2. **The scope-and-figure rule.** Sector numbers diverge by definition: "senior
   housing" vs "IRC" vs "retirement housing"; AU resident counts vary from
   209k to 228k by source; ARCO's £40bn and NHS savings figures are advocacy
   numbers. Every figure carries source, year and scope. Never an ARCO or
   operator claim in our own voice.
3. **The resident-harm rule (new).** This sector's bad news is about old
   people losing money or care: repayment waits, fee disputes, insolvencies
   (e.g. the Purley scheme developer's administration), fires (a 16-death
   senior living fire in the US wire this week), abuse cases. We cover
   insolvencies, fee rulings and regulation as business and regulatory risk. We
   do not cover individual deaths, abuse, crime or safety incidents as news,
   and we never name private residents.
4. **The no-advice rule.** No financial, legal or care advice to consumers.
   Event fees and DMFs are explained from the operator's P&L side. This keeps
   the title clear of YMYL exposure, which would otherwise be real (retirement
   finance for the elderly is a textbook YMYL topic).

### Visual requirement

Closer to golf than airports: architecture and landscape photography of the
communities, with data tables for the trackers. Must not look like a
consumer lifestyle site (the contamination risk is visual as well as
editorial).
