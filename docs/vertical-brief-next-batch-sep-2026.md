## 1. Energy: yes, but only as the installer's business title

### The scope decision comes first

"Energy" as briefed is not a title, it is four titles, and three of them are already ours.
The fleet and the legacy book already occupy most of the energy ground:

| Energy ground | Already owned by |
|---|---|
| Energy costs and bills for a small business | Smart SME (`lib/news-searches.js:108`, live wire) |
| Depot charging, EV infrastructure, fleet electrification | The Fleet Magazine (`batch-plan-fleet-magazine.json:70`) |
| Solar and battery land leases, farm energy income | Smart Farming News (`battery-storage-lease-rents`, parked brief) |
| Airport energy, SAF, net zero | Airport Business (`lib/news-searches.js:568`) |
| Industrial and manufacturing energy, power, automation | MEPCA |

The one seam none of them touches, and no incumbent covers either, is **the contractor who
installs the kit, read as a business**: heat pumps, solar, batteries, EV chargers,
insulation and retrofit. Pricing, margin, quoting, lead cost, MCS and scheme compliance
cost, hiring, finance, and getting paid by scheme administrators.

### What the title actually covers

The reader is the **owner or director of a firm that installs energy kit in other people's
buildings**: a three-van heat pump outfit, a twenty-person solar EPC, an electrical
contractor adding batteries and chargers, a retrofit coordinator. Not the homeowner, not the
manufacturer, not the utility.

Proposed sections:

| Section | What sits in it |
|---|---|
| **Money** | Margin and pricing, quoting and win rates, lead cost and where leads come from, subcontracting rates, cash flow, chasing scheme payments, finance and asset finance, insurance and warranty cost |
| **Schemes** | Boiler Upgrade Scheme, Warm Homes Plan and Warm Homes Local Grant, ECO4 and its successor, Great British Energy programmes, Smart Export Guarantee, how each one pays and how late |
| **Compliance** | MCS, RECC and HIES, PAS 2035 and 2030, Part P and Part L, gas and F-gas, what certification costs and what it costs to lose it |
| **People** | Hiring and keeping installers, apprenticeships and training routes, subcontractor versus employee, pay rates, the skills shortage as a cost line |
| **Kit** | Manufacturers, inverters, batteries, monitoring and job-management software, judged on payback, warranty terms and channel support, never on specification for its own sake |
| **Growth** | Winning commercial and social housing work, framework and tender routes, buying and selling an installation business, roll-ups and consolidators |
| **Openings and closures** | Who started, who expanded, who went under and why. This is the beat, not a sidebar |

Out of scope, and the scope rule has to say so explicitly: consumer buying advice ("should I
get a heat pump"), installation how-to, energy prices and tariffs for households, utility and
grid politics, corporate net zero and ESG reporting. Each of those is either someone else's
title or one of ours.

### Risk

1. **Four-way internal collision.** The most collision-prone vertical we have looked at.
   Claimed terms from Smart SME, Fleet, Farming, Airports and MEPCA must go into
   `KeywordTarget` before anything is commissioned, and MEPCA's are invisible to the
   registry today.
2. **Consumer mis-selling adjacency.** Solar mis-selling, green finance and ECO4 fraud are
   live stories. A business title reporting them is fine; a business title that reads as
   consumer advice is not. Needs the fleet's standard attribution rule plus a
   "we cover the business of installing, never consumer buying advice" scope rule.
3. **YMYL-lite.** Grant eligibility and finance affect readers' money. Attribute scheme
   figures to gov.uk and date them; never state a grant rate as fact in evergreen copy.
4. **Name collision.** "Installer" is Installer Online's brand and their show. Avoid it.

### Naming and domains

`energyinstallerbusiness.com`, `retrofitbusinessnews.com`, `theretrofitmagazine.com`,
`solarbusinessnews.com`, `heatpumpbusinessnews.com`, `renewableinstaller.com`,

**Recommendation: Energy Installer Business (`energyinstallerbusiness.com`).** It names the

Installer Online's brand. Second choice **Retrofit Business News**, rejected as first choice
only because "retrofit" excludes new-build solar and EV charging, which is money we want.

## 2. FinTech: no

### The incumbent field is roughly twice as strong as the strongest we have ever faced

Tranco, 24 Sep 2026: **Finextra 26,824**, **Sifted 39,215** (FT-backed), **FinTech Futures
79,932**, **The Fintech Times 96,918**, AltFi 434,398.

Farmers Weekly at 50,669 was the ceiling we said we would not attack head on. Finextra is
twice that, Sifted has the Financial Times behind it, and two more sit under 100k. There is
no weak seam underneath them, because the trade press, the banks, the comparison sites and
the FCA itself all publish into the same SERPs.

### The format does not fit

Every title that works for us is an **operator** title: a gym owner, a nursery owner, a
farmer, a dentist, a barber. The seam is always "the business of running one of these".
FinTech has no operator tail. It is a supplier sector of startups and banks, already served
by five publishers, two of them venture-funded.

### Regulation is the worst case for an AI-assisted engine

The UK financial promotions regime is the strictest it has ever been: the s.21 FSMA approver
gateway is in steady state and the Consumer Duty layers an outcomes test over every
promotion. Article 20 of the Financial Promotion Order gives journalism an exemption, so a
title is publishable, but any commercial page, any sponsored piece and any product comparison
sits close to the line. Finance is also the hardest YMYL category in search, which is the
single worst place to put a machine-drafted engine.

### Collision

SME finance, funding, payments and lending are Smart SME's existing ground. A fintech title
would cannibalise it immediately, and the registry would not stop it because the overlap is
conceptual rather than term-for-term.

### Scored

Advertisers 16, incumbents 3, search 3, money 10, readers 6, news 9, CIM asset 2, risk 3.
**Total 52**, below every title we have launched.

Compare the fintech field in §2 (Finextra 26,824, Sifted 39,215). This one sits in the same
band as the airport incumbents we did attack, and the UK mid-market title, Real Deals, is not

### The closest analogue runs our exact business model, and it is small

**Hedgeweek and Private Equity Wire are both free, ad and sponsorship funded, and owned by one
small publisher, Global Fund Media.** Combined they report **30,000 newsletter subscribers**
and six annual conferences of about 500 senior executives each. That is proof the
free-plus-newsletter-plus-sponsorship model works against this audience, at a subscriber count
our drip reaches in thirty weeks.

It is also the warning: **the revenue in this market is events and subscriptions, not display.**
Global Fund Media's six conferences are the engine. We do not run events. Anyone judging this
title on traffic-and-display economics will call it a failure while it is working.

### The seam, and it is one nobody else can copy

**Cogent already publishes in six of the sectors UK lower-mid-market private equity is actively
rolling up:** gyms, nurseries, dental practices, senior living, veterinary-adjacent care,
farming, barbering. The fleet's own coverage is proprietary deal intelligence about the buy
side of exactly those sectors, and no PE title has it.

So the seam is **the lower-mid-market roll-up beat**: who is buying UK SME operators, at what
multiple, funded by whom, and what happens to the businesses afterwards. PEI and With
Intelligence serve the mega-funds because that is where subscription money is. The tail below
them, independent sponsors, search funds, sub-£100m funds, family offices and boutique credit,
is underserved for the same reason the gym tail was.

Second seam, and the one that carries search: **running the firm rather than running the money.**
Fund administration costs, regulatory reporting, LLP and salaried member rules, comp and
carry structures, outsourcing, fund tech. Those queries are held by law firms and the Big
Four, not by publishers, which is our standard pattern.

### Live proof the compliance seam has demand

The **UK carried interest regime changed on 6 April 2026**: carried interest is now trading
profit subject to income tax and Class 4 NICs, with a 72.5% multiplier on qualifying carry
giving an effective rate near 34.1%, and a 40-month holding test deciding what qualifies.
Every UK carry holder needs this explained. The explainers currently ranking are Grant
Thornton, BDO, RSM, Mayer Brown, Saffery and Latham. No trade title owns it.

### Reader universe: the smallest viable one we have measured

UK Private Capital (renamed from the BVCA on 27 Jan 2026) has **600+ member firms: 250+ private
equity and venture capital houses, 100+ institutional investors, 200+ professional services
firms**. The industry backs 13,000 UK businesses, employs 2.5 million people and is **7% of
GDP**, with £29.4bn invested in 2024. Add hedge funds, credit funds and family offices and the
addressable firm count is perhaps 1,500. Against 5,250 MCS contractors that is small, but the
value per reader is the highest in the fleet by an order of magnitude.

### Why the risk row is much better than §2 suggested

The §2 rejection was partly built on consumer YMYL and the financial promotions regime. Neither
bites as hard here. The readers are **professional clients and eligible counterparties**, not
retail, so FPO article 19 applies alongside the article 20 journalism exemption, and we would be
writing about fund operations and deal activity rather than giving investment advice.

The real risk is different and sharper: **this is the most credibility-sensitive audience we
would ever serve.** One wrong multiple, one misstated tax rate, one invented quote and the title
is finished with people who all know each other. That argues for a hard sourcing rule and for
leaning on the people-led format from day one.

4. **Check Smart SME collision.** "Selling your business", "buying a business uk" and exits

## 3. Cannabis: no, and this one has a tail risk the others do not

### The search is genuinely open. That is not the problem

Tranco, 24 Sep 2026: Business of Cannabis 888,445, Prohibition Partners 995,904, Cannabis
Health News 1,571,436, Leafie 1,917,917. Weaker than any field we have attacked.

But **Business of Cannabis, Cannabis Health and the report series are all one company,
Prohibition Partners**, which also runs the events and claims 80,000+ subscribers. They are
weak in search and strong everywhere that gets paid. Taking the SERP would not take the
market.

### The reader universe is the smallest we have measured

UK medical cannabis runs almost entirely outside the NHS through **40+ private clinics, of
which 8 platforms issue an estimated 80% of prescriptions**. Market value is £302m to £398m
for 2026 with a base case near £345m, against 60,000 to 75,000 active patients. Compare
5,842 gyms, 5,250 MCS contractors or 168,000 English farm principals. There are perhaps a
few dozen genuine B2B buyers in the UK.

### You cannot sell the advertising

Medical cannabis products are prescription-only and unlicensed. HMR 2012 prohibits promoting
prescription-only medicines to the public, and unlicensed CBPMs cannot be promoted in any way
likely to lead to prescription outside an unsolicited clinical decision. Clinics may advertise
their services, which leaves clinic ads on a public website as close to the entire addressable
inventory, drawn from a pool of forty, eight of which matter.

### The reason it is a no rather than a low-scoring maybe

**A cannabis title puts the shared fleet infrastructure at risk for the other ten titles.**

- **Mailchimp's Acceptable Use Policy prohibits cannabis, hemp and CBD content, and Mailchimp
  has terminated accounts for it.** One Mailchimp account holds every title's audience. A
  cannabis audience in it risks the account that sends Smart SME, MEPCA, Fleet, Golf,
  Barbering, Airport, Gym, Nursery, Senior and Dental.
- Google Ads and Meta both restrict the category, which touches Site Kit, GA4 and any paid
  distribution.
- The LinkedIn app (266128203) is in Community Management API review. A cannabis title on the
  same app is an unforced risk to a review that is already the bottleneck for ten titles.
- Make.com, Apollo and payment processors all carry category restrictions.

If it ever ran it would need its own ESP, its own ad accounts, its own LinkedIn app and its
own everything, which is the opposite of the fleet model and removes every economy that makes
a Cogent title cost what it costs.
