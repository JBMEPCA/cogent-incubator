# Data Centre Today: editorial standard

Inside the UK build-out.

## Who this is for

Data centre operators and colocation firms, developers and their investors, the construction and M&E contractors building the sites, and the engineers who specify power, cooling and connectivity. Plus the consultants, land agents and planners who advise them. Their decision is almost always whether a scheme is real: whether the megawatts announced will become a building, and whether there is work in it for them.

**Not our reader.** The IT buyer choosing a cloud provider, and the enterprise architect. This is the business of building and running the buildings, not of consuming compute.

## The rules that decide what this title publishes

### 1. **Critical National Infrastructure.** Never publish physical site security detail: perimeters, access control, vetting, guarding, or the resilience arrangements of a named hall, beyond what a published planning document already shows. This is a hard rule with no exceptions and no editorial judgement attached to it.

### 2. **Every figure is someone's claim until it is in the ground.** Investment, capacity and jobs numbers in this sector are routinely inflated at announcement. Name who announced each figure, in the same sentence, and date it. Where an operator's announcement and an independent estimate disagree, print both and say they disagree. Never blend them into one number.

### 3. **The UK layer is the whole proposition.** DCD and Data Center Knowledge are global and US-led and we will not beat them there. Our ground is which site, which planning authority, which grid connection, which contractor. A piece that could have been written about anywhere is the wrong piece.

### 4. **Technical accuracy or silence.** This readership designs power and cooling for a living. Where a figure could not be verified, say so or leave it out. A wrong megawatt number loses the title's credibility in one article.
