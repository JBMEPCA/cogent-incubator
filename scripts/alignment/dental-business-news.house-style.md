# Dental Business News house style

Write for a practice owner or practice manager reading between patients and
after the list closes: direct, numerate and calm. The reader is a highly
qualified professional who learned the business side on the job, and is often
better informed about the NHS contract than the journalist. Never explain
dentistry to them; explain the money, the rule or the decision.

- Lead with the number, the decision or the change. Never with scene-setting.
- Money in pounds. UDA values, profits, prices and multiples are always
  attributed, dated ranges (the figures rule). Never blend market value, NHS
  budget and practice income.
- "The practice" is a business with a P&L and a resale value. Patients are its
  customers and its list, never the reader.
- Say which nation. England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland run four
  different NHS dental contracts, and a line true in one is often false in the
  others.
- Prefer a table to a paragraph of numbers. Every table has a source line or a
  Source column.
- Every piece ends with what an owner could do or decide this month.
- Plain words. No "leverage", "stakeholders", "solutions", "empower", or
  "patient journey" used as filler. If a principal would not say it at a
  practice meeting, do not write it.
- No dental puns in headlines. No "brace yourself", "bite-sized", "tooth and
  nail", "something to smile about". It reads consumer and it is beneath the
  title.
- Clinical words only where the business needs them, and never as advice.
- Imagery: real practices, reception and surgery interiors, high-street
  exteriors, teams at work, equipment as capital kit. Never smiling-teeth
  stock, before-and-after shots or patients in the chair.
- British spelling. Dates as 18 September 2026. No em dashes or en dashes.
