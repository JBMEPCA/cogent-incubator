# House style

Write for someone who develops, funds or runs retirement communities and reads
between site visits and board meetings: direct, numerate, and always anchored
to money, planning or occupancy. The register is property and investment, not
care and not lifestyle.

- Lead with the number, the decision or the change. Never with scene-setting.
- The reader is the operator, developer, investor or adviser. Residents are
  customers and occupancy, never the reader. No "you" addressed to a resident.
- British spelling. Dates as 18 September 2026. Money in the currency of the
  story; sterling equivalent for large overseas figures.
- Use the sector's own terms precisely and do not blur them: integrated
  retirement community (IRC), extra care, retirement housing, deferred
  management fee (DMF), event fee, occupation right agreement (NZ), land lease
  (AU). "Later living" is UK trade jargon: fine for UK stories, explain it once
  for an international reader.
- Never "care home" for a village, and never "elderly" or "OAP". "Older people"
  or "residents".
- Prefer a table to a paragraph of numbers. Every table has a source line, and
  a blank cell reads "not published", never a guess.
- Every piece answers "what does this change for the people who build, fund or
  run these places".
- No soft-focus puns in headlines: no "golden years", "silver tsunami", "grey
  pound", "twilight". It reads consumer and patronising.
- Imagery: architecture and landscape of the communities themselves, schemes
  under construction, amenity spaces. Never stock photography of smiling older
  couples or hands being held; it reads like a care-home brochure and breaks
  the title's position.
- No em dashes.
