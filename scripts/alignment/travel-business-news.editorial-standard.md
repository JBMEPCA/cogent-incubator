# Travel Business News: editorial standard

The business of getting there.

## Who this is for

Corporate travel managers, finance and operations directors, procurement leads, PAs and office managers, and the SME owners who book their own travel. They are spending an employer's money and running a programme: the decisions are what the policy says, what a trip is allowed to cost, who is liable when something goes wrong, and which supplier or tool to buy.

**Not our reader.** The holidaymaker, and the travel agent. Leisure travel is not this title's subject, and the agency and tour operator trade belongs to Travel Weekly and TTG. A reader planning a holiday has arrived at the wrong publication.

## The rules that decide what this title publishes

### 1. **Business travel, not travel.** No holidays, no destinations, no package or cruise trade, no leisure agency news, no travel inspiration. The reader is spending their employer's money and managing a programme. This angle was chosen deliberately over the agency trade, which the incumbents own.

### 2. **Never give tax or legal advice in our own voice.** This beat runs on HMRC rules, employer duties under health and safety law, and visa and entry requirements, all of which carry real consequences when wrong. Attribute every rule to the named HMRC manual page, the Act, the regulation or the regulator, give the date it was checked, and send the reader to that source and to their own adviser.

### 3. **Rates and thresholds go stale and the stale ones are everywhere.** Vendor blogs still publish HMRC subsistence rates that were retired in 2016. Never quote a rate without naming the HMRC page and its update date, and where an old figure is still circulating, show it beside the current one so the reader can see which is which.

### 4. **The suppliers are the advertisers and they are not the sources.** TMCs, booking and expense platforms and travel risk firms all publish explainers that exist to sell. They may be named and quoted as interested parties; they are never the authority for a rule or a figure.
