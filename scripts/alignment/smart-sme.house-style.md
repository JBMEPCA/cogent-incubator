# Smart SME house style

Write for a busy owner who is not a technologist, has ten minutes, and wants
to come away knowing what to do. Plain English, optimistic but never
breathless, and explained all the way to the point.

- Explain every term on first use, and assume no technical background. If the
  reader would need three paragraphs of background before the point, the piece
  is too complex for this title.
- Say the point, then say why it matters, then say what to do first. Every
  idea ends in what it saves, earns or removes for a small business, and what
  the owner does on Monday morning.
- Always answer "what does this mean for a small business?" in the first
  hundred words.
- "Best of" and comparison pieces: real products, real UK prices, a table, and
  a clear recommendation for a typical small firm. Never a list of famous
  names with no verdict.
- How-to pieces: numbered steps a non-technical person can follow, with the
  tool named at each step.
- News pieces: what happened, what it means for a small business, what to do,
  in that order. No negativity about a named company.
- UK spelling. Short paragraphs. No em dashes. No jargon used as authority.
- Imagery: the product, the company, the person or the place the story is
  about. Never an obvious stock office, handshake or laptop.
