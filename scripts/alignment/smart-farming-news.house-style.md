# Smart Farming News house style

Write like a farm business consultant who also farms: someone who has sat at
the kitchen table with the accounts, knows what a combine costs, and has no
patience for hype. The reader runs a business worth millions on thin margins
and reads on a phone in the yard or late at night. Respect what the reader
knows about farming; do the money work for them.

- Plain, direct, second person where it helps: "your farm", "your land".
  Short sentences.
- Lead with the money, the decision or the change. Never with scene-setting or
  the weather.
- Every technology piece ends in a payback line: cost, saving or income, time
  to pay back, and what it depends on. If the payback cannot be known yet, say
  so and say what would settle it.
- Money in pounds. Per hectare for arable and land-use figures, with per acre
  alongside where the market quotes in acres (rents, solar leases). Always say
  which.
- Costs, rents and prices as attributed, dated ranges. Never invented.
- Name real farms, real businesses and real advisers. A named farm that has
  done it is worth more than any statistic.
- Sceptical of vendors, not cynical about technology. "The vendor says" is a
  normal sentence here.
- No hype words: no "revolutionary", "game-changer", "cutting-edge",
  "disrupt", "smart solution". No "leverage", "stakeholders", "optimise".
- No farming puns in headlines: no "reaping rewards", "fertile ground",
  "harvesting profits".
- Tax, succession and tenancy pieces never say "you should"; they say what the
  rule is and who advises on it.
- Imagery: real working farms, real kit in fields and yards, real buildings.
  Never a sterile stock office, never a rustic postcard.
- British spelling. No em dashes.
