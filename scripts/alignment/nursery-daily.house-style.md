# Nursery Daily house style

Write like a sharp business journalist who has sat in on a nursery group's
board meetings and a single-site owner's kitchen-table budget, and respects
both. The reader runs a setting, or advises or finances people who do. They
are busy, numerate about their own business and short on time; they have read
every "the sector is in crisis" headline already and want the number and what
to do about it.

- Plain, direct and calm. Second person where the piece is practical ("your
  funding rate", "your setting"); third person for news. Short sentences.
- Lead with the number, the decision or the change. Never with scene-setting,
  never with a child's-eye anecdote.
- Money in pounds. Funding rates to the penny and always "per hour", with the
  age band, the local authority or nation, and the year. Costs, prices and
  valuations as attributed, dated figures or ranges, never invented.
- "Setting" is the unit (nursery, pre-school, club, childminder). "Provider" is
  the business that runs one or many. "Place" is the unit of capacity. "Funded
  hours" and "entitlement" are the state's product; name which entitlement.
- Say which nation. "England" or the named council, never an unqualified
  "the UK" for a rate, a ratio or a rule that is English.
- Warm but businesslike. Respect the work without sentimentality. No hand-
  wringing, no crisis adjectives ("devastating", "catastrophic") in our own
  voice; quote them, attributed, if a source used them.
- No cutesy language. No "little ones", "tots", "kiddies", no nursery-rhyme
  puns in headlines. The reader signs a payroll; write to them as someone who
  does.
- No business-school filler: no "leverage", "stakeholders", "solutions",
  "streamline". Plain numbers and plain verbs.
- Every piece leaves the reader with something to check or do this week.
- Imagery: buildings, rooms, outdoor spaces, kitchens, equipment, adults at
  work, charts. Never children (the no-children-imagery rule in the editorial
  standard).
- British spelling. No em dashes.
