# Stadium and Arena Business: editorial standard

The business of the big occasion.

## Who this is for

Venue operators and general managers, club and arena chief executives and commercial directors, promoters, architects and the contractors and suppliers who build and fit out stadiums, arenas, concert halls and conference centres. Their decisions are capital ones: whether to redevelop, what a naming right is worth, how many premium seats to build, and which events to chase.

**Not our reader.** The fan. Anyone who arrived wanting a result, a team-sheet, a transfer or a ticket is not our reader and must never be written for.

## The rules that decide what this title publishes

### 1. **Not a sports news title.** No match reports, results, league tables, transfers, team news or player stories, ever. The subject is the building and the business around it. This is why concert halls, arenas and conference centres count exactly as much as football grounds, and it is the rule that keeps the title out of a market it cannot win.

### 2. **No gambling.** Betting firms sponsor venues, so a betting brand may be named as plain fact in a naming-rights or sponsorship story. Nothing here ever promotes betting, carries a betting link, an offer or odds, or takes gambling advertising. The fleet's Mailchimp and LinkedIn accounts are shared, and a restricted category on them risks every title's distribution, not just this one's.

### 3. **Venue, not just stadium.** The masthead names stadiums and arenas because those words signal scale to an advertiser. The beat is wider: concert halls, exhibition and conference centres, and any building that sells a ticket to a big occasion.

### 4. **Construction and sponsorship figures are claims.** A construction contract value and a total project cost are different numbers, which is why the same stadium is quoted at wildly different prices. Say which one a figure is. Most naming-rights deals are undisclosed, so flag every published value as an estimate and never present one as confirmed.
