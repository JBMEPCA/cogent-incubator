# Smart Farming News editorial standard

Written at launch prep, 18 Sep 2026, from docs/vertical-brief-farming.md. To be
re-aligned when the title's owner returns the Title Alignment Sheet. The angle
is "farming smarter as a business": technology judged by its payback, plus the
land-business money (energy leases, natural capital, diversification, buildings,
tax and succession rules). Where this standard and a general business instinct
differ, this standard wins.

## The money-question rule, which outranks everything else here

Every piece answers three questions for a farm or land business: what does it
cost, what does it return, and who has done it. A robot, a software package, a
solar lease, a habitat bank and a holiday let are all capital decisions, and the
article is not finished until the reader can see the payback, or the reason it
cannot yet be known. If a piece cannot name a cost, a return or a real farm or
business that has done it, it is a press release and does not run.

Not our reader, even though they land on the site: shoppers, tourists, farm-shop
visitors, Clarkson's Farm viewers, smallholding dreamers and hobby farmers. No
farm-shop reviews, no days out, no property listings, no celebrity farming.

## The no-advice rule (tax, inheritance, succession, tenancies and leases)

APR and BPR, inheritance tax, succession, tenancy terms and energy or BNG lease
terms are high-value money decisions, often on estates worth millions. We
report the rules, the numbers, the deadlines and named advisers' views. We never
tell a reader what to do with their estate, their tenancy or their land, and
never write "you should" on a tax or succession point. Every tax piece names the
rule's source (HMRC, the Finance Act, the Commons Library) and its date, and
says the reader should take professional advice. When the rules change, the
piece is re-reviewed.

The farm inheritance tax change is politically charged. We take no line on it:
no view on the government, the campaigns, the protests or the reversal pressure.
We report who pays, how much, from when, and what named advisers say.

## The claims rule (vendor and yield claims)

Technology vendors, energy developers, carbon and BNG brokers and lead-gen
sites all publish yields, savings, rents and paybacks. Every such figure is
attributed to whoever claimed it, dated, and never asserted in our own voice.
"Up to 30% less spray" is the vendor's claim, written as the vendor's claim.
Where independent data exists (AHDB, Defra, a university trial, a land agent's
survey), set it beside the claim. Lease rents, BNG unit prices and carbon prices
are always quoted as attributed ranges: definitions differ between sources, and
a single number in our voice is wrong for most readers.

## The stay-off-the-daily-beat rule

Commodity prices, general SFI and Countryside Stewardship news, livestock
disease, machinery launches for their own sake and contractor charges are the
incumbents' daily ground, and they will always have it first. We cover them only
when the story changes a payback sum or a land-use decision, and the headline
says which. The engine is never seeded with the bare queries "farmers",
"farm shop", "farm for sale" or "farm subsidies".

## The politics rule

Solar-on-farmland fights, planning objections and "food versus energy" rows are
reported as business: who is paid, how much, what was decided and why. No
editorial line on whether farmland should host solar.

## The farm-incident rule

Farm deaths, rural crime and farmer mental health appear in the feed daily. We
do not cover fatalities or crime as news. Mental health is covered only as a
business-support topic with named charities, never as human-interest copy.

## Our other titles

Vehicle tax, pickups and double-cab benefit-in-kind belong to a sister title:
cover farm vehicles only as farm capex, and never target pickup or BIK queries.
Generic small-business queries (how to start a shop, business rates for any
business) belong to a sister title: take them only where the angle is
specifically the farm or the landowner.

## Events and pegs

LAMMA (NEC, January), Cereals (June), Groundswell (June/July), the Royal
Highland and Great Yorkshire Shows (June/July), the Farm Business Innovation
Show (NEC, November) and the Budget (November). Scheduled data drops: the Defra
Farm Business Survey diversification chapter and Farm Business Income, Total
Income from Farming, AEA tractor registrations (monthly), Savills and the land
agents' farmland surveys, Farm Rents statistics, Woodland Carbon Code prices.

## Standing rules

- Named bylines on everything.
- Never invent a statistic, a source, a quote, a farm or a commentator.
- Link out to the company, the scheme page and the original announcement on any
  news piece.
- No em dashes or en dashes, per house style.

## The headline length rule

Google shows about 60 characters of a headline. The news goes in the first 60
characters: who, what happened, and the number if there is one. Aim for 60
characters before the colon; 65 is the ceiling.
