# Editorial standard

Written from docs/vertical-brief-retirement-villages.md on 18 September 2026,
before launch. No title alignment sheet exists yet; replace or extend this
when the title's owner returns one.

This title covers the retirement community as a business: development
pipeline, capital and investors, planning (the C2/C3 use-class fight), operator
economics, event fees and deferred management fees, sales and marketing, design
and amenities, and the people who run villages. UK-led, with Australia and New
Zealand as the second market because they share the deferred-fee model, and the
US only for capital deals and benchmarks.

## The front-door rule, which decides whether a story is ours at all

If the resident has their own front door and holds a lease, licence or
tenancy, the story is ours: retirement villages, integrated retirement
communities, extra care, assisted living, housing with care, retirement
housing. If the resident has a room and a care plan in a CQC-registered care
home (residential, nursing or dementia care), it belongs to CIM's sister title
Care Home Magazine, and we do not write it.

- Care home development, planning and deals: not ours, even when the developer
  is also a village developer.
- CQC inspections and ratings, care workforce, pay and strikes, clinical
  practice: not ours.
- A mixed campus (village plus care home): we cover the development, the
  capital and the village operation; the care home's operation, ratings and
  staffing are not ours.
- Domiciliary care delivered inside a village: only as an operating model and a
  cost line.
- In Australia, "aged care" means residential aged care, which is the other
  side of the front door. The same rule applies.

This is a commercial rule as well as an editorial one. Care Home Magazine is
CIM's, its advertisers are ours to cross-sell, and competing with it for its own
readers or keywords would cost both titles. We never target a search query
containing "care home", "nursing home" or "residential care".

## The operator rule

Every article is written for someone who is paid to develop, fund, run, design,
supply or regulate a retirement community: operator, developer, investor,
lender, planner, architect, council commissioner, adviser, supplier. If the
natural reader is a resident or their family, it is not our article. No "how to
choose a retirement village", no "is it worth it", no fee explainers written
for buyers. Resident complaints are covered as regulatory and reputational risk
to operators, never as advice to residents.

## The no-advice rule

No financial, legal or care advice to consumers, ever. Event fees and deferred
management fees are explained from the operator's P&L. Planning law is
described, and the reader is pointed to counsel. Retirement finance for older
people is a textbook your-money-or-your-life topic, and a trade title with no
regulated adviser has no standing to give it.

## The resident-harm rule

This sector's bad news is about older people losing money or care: repayment
waits, fee disputes, insolvencies, fires, abuse. We cover insolvencies, fee
rulings, tribunal decisions and regulation as business and regulatory risk. We
do not cover individual deaths, abuse, crime or safety incidents as news, and
we never name a private resident. If a story's hook is that a resident was
hurt, it is not our story.

## The scope-and-figure rule

Sector numbers diverge by definition: "senior housing", "integrated retirement
community" and "retirement housing" are different stock; Australian resident
counts vary by source. ARCO's investment and turnover projections and every
operator's savings claim are advocacy figures. Every figure carries source,
year and scope, or is quoted as a range with the sources named. Never an ARCO
or operator claim in our own voice. Money in the currency of the market, with
sterling for large overseas figures.

## Events and pegs

ARCO What Next? (July), Housing LIN annual conference (February or March),
Property Week Later Living and Care (March), Retirement Living and Care UK
(December, London), the NZ Retirement Villages Association conference
(August), the Property Council of Australia's National Retirement Living
Summit. Scheduled data drops: Knight Frank's seniors housing reviews, NIC MAP
quarterly occupancy, and the half-year and full-year results of Ryman,
Summerset, Oceania, Ingenia, Stockland and the other listed operators. Cover
events before they happen; exhibitor and sponsor lists are advertiser
prospects, and speakers are interview targets.

## Standing rules

- Named bylines on everything.
- Never invent a statistic, a source, a quote or a commentator.
- Link out to the organisation and the original announcement on any news piece.
- No em dashes or en dashes, per house style.

## The headline length rule

Google shows about 60 characters of a headline. Everything past that is cut off
and the reader never sees it.

So the news goes in the first 60 characters: who, what happened, and the number
if there is one. The explainer clause after the colon is written for somebody
already on the page, and nothing load-bearing belongs in it.

Aim for 60 characters before the colon. Sixty-five is the ceiling. A headline
that needs more than that is usually two stories or one unmade decision.
