Independent supplier comparisons for the systems and services airports actually buy: de-icing, baggage handling, common-use passenger processing, biometric boarding, terminal construction, retail concessions and route development. Each guide covers the vendors, the contract models and the cost drivers, and this page is updated as new comparisons are published.

Winter operations and de-icing

De-icing is the clearest example of a capital decision driven by a forecasting problem. Start with our comparison of de-icing vehicle suppliers, covering Vestergaard, Overaasen, Boschung and others, then read de-icing fleet management software compared on forecasting and fleet tooling, because the fleet you need depends on how well you can predict the event.

That forecasting gap is now a documented regulatory concern: the EASA atmospheric icing report sets out the winter ops forecasting gaps airports must budget for. Funding can offset some of the capital cost, as Ontario International winning a $4.15 million FAA VALE grant shows.

Baggage and passenger processing

Baggage is the longest-lived system decision most terminals make. Our integrator comparison covering Vanderlande, Beumer and the rest sets out how the major players differ on scope and risk transfer.

On the passenger side, CUPPS vendors and costs compared covers common-use processing, and biometric boarding vendors compared on cost and capability covers what is realistically deployable now. Both sit inside a security experience that is being actively reworked: see AirportsUK crediting checkpoint investment for rising screening satisfaction and the Malaysia Airports six-agency security reset.
