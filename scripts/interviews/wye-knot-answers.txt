You opened Wye Knot in Builth Wells on 29 June with no previous retail experience. What made you take the leap, and what surprised you most in the first few weeks?
Living in Wales had been a dream since childhood, and I had wanted to own a gift shop for around six years. Ian and I had visited the Builth Wells area regularly before we moved here in November 2025. When premises on the High Street became available, I already knew the owners, and the chance to open a shop felt like the right moment to bring those two hopes together. I had worked as a school cleaner at Sundorne Academy in Shrewsbury, so retail was a completely new direction. With help from family and friends, we transformed the shop in two weeks. I wanted it to have personality and to be full of things that might make someone smile or start a conversation. I was surprised by how quickly customers challenged my assumptions and how warmly neighbouring businesses welcomed us.

You expected Welsh sheep and dragon gifts to lead sales, but Highland cow products sold more strongly. When did you notice, and what did you change as a result?
I started to notice the difference once customers began choosing from the opening range. I had expected Welsh sheep and dragon gifts to be our strongest themes, but Highland cow products performed better than I anticipated. It was a useful reminder that my own ideas are only a starting point; the people coming through the door show me what resonates. I am using those early sales to guide what I consider reordering, while looking for demand that continues rather than reacting to one busy day. I still want Wye Knot to reflect its Welsh setting, but I am learning to keep the mix open and let customers’ choices shape it. Cards have also surprised me: requests for Welsh-language birthday cards led us to add 65 designs.

Now that you have real sales to go on, how do you decide what to reorder, what to try and what to drop?
I look at what people actually buy, how quickly a product sells, whether demand continues and what is still on the shelf. I try not to make a decision based on a single comment or one busy day. When I consider something new, I think about quality, value, presentation and personality, as well as supplier minimum orders and delivery times. If a product is not moving, I need to consider how much stock remains and whether it makes sense to reorder before deciding what to do next. Wye Knot is still a new shop, so I am learning which ranges have lasting demand. My aim is to use customer feedback alongside purchases, and keep some money available for reordering products people have shown they want.

Every new shop has to balance buying enough stock against keeping money back for reorders. How have you handled that, and what would you do differently?
I began with smaller quantities across different ranges, so I could offer variety without tying too much money up in one assumption. Supplier minimum orders affect how small some tests can be, so there is a balance between having enough choice and protecting cash. As the first sales came in, I saw how important it is to keep working capital available for the products customers show they want. Looking back, I would keep more of the initial budget back for reordering once I had evidence from real purchases. With no previous year’s figures, I could not know exactly what would sell before opening. I now see the first order as a starting point: buy thoughtfully, watch what happens, and leave enough room to respond.

You have added 65 greeting card designs, and Welsh birthday cards are especially popular. Why lean into cards, and what have they done for the shop?
Cards have become an important part of Wye Knot because customers asked for them and then showed us what they wanted. Requests for Welsh-language birthday cards prompted us to add 65 designs. I have also found that many people prefer cards that are blank inside, because they want to write a message in their own words. I like that a card can feel personal, and I wanted the shop to offer things that might make someone smile or start a conversation. The response has helped us build a range that reflects what customers are actually looking for, rather than just my own taste. It has also made me think about whether, in the longer term, a broader greetings selection might need more space. That remains an ambition, not a confirmed move.

You launched an online shop in mid-August, alongside the physical one. What works differently online, and has it been worth the effort so far?
The High Street shop lets people pick things up, browse in person and ask me about a gift. Online, customers need clear information and photographs to help them decide, along with straightforward delivery or collection details. The website gives people another way to browse our selection, with UK delivery and free collection available on eligible orders. We launched in mid-August, so the online shop is still new and I am developing it alongside the physical one. I think it is worth continuing because it gives people another way to find Wye Knot, including those who cannot visit Builth Wells. I do not yet have a full year of results, so I am not making claims about its sales impact; I am learning what works as we go.

What would you tell someone opening their first shop next year, and what is next for Wye Knot?
I would tell them that it is all right to learn as you go. Choose your opening range thoughtfully, but do not expect to know exactly what customers will want until they have had the chance to show you. Start with quantities that leave room to adjust, pay attention to what people actually buy, and remember that supplier minimums and delivery times affect how quickly you can respond. I have also learned how much practical help matters: family and friends helped us prepare the shop, and neighbouring businesses have recommended us and sent customers our way. For Wye Knot, the immediate focus is our first Christmas, while continuing to develop the online shop. A larger premises with more room for cards is something I may explore in future, but there is no confirmed move.
